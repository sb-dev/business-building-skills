## 1. Reconciliation rule

First preserve each author's actual position, including qualifications that complicate a convenient contrast. Then distinguish semantic disagreement, different business contexts, uncertain causal claims and conflicts with an already governing project constraint. Resolve only what source comparison and the bootstrap contract permit. Questions requiring broader evidence remain explicit Stage 3 research inputs, as §9 requires; they are not silently answered by memory.

The provisional synthesis is a connected business-decision discipline: customer and value evidence inform offers and prices; acquisition is assessed through suitable conversion and delivered value; revenue mechanisms carry cost, capacity and cash obligations; experiments inform bounded commitment; diagnosis preserves accepted decisions except where evidence implicates them. This is a project synthesis of the corpus and Stage 1, not a quotation or a claim that all five books prescribe the same system.

Author claims, reported examples and independently verified observations are different evidence types. Repeated advice in O/L/M is particularly dependent because the books share an author and business history. The Personal MBA is itself a synthesis and draws on adjacent traditions; The Lean Startup acknowledges prior lean and customer-development work. Agreement can justify investigating a reusable capability, not asserting an independently replicated effect.

## 2. Capability discovery and provisional taxonomy

The candidate matrix was built from source concepts and their qualifications, then consolidated by the business decision supported rather than by book, executive role or a fixed skill count. The table below defines the resulting responsibilities. Its workflow/check/case columns implement the §3 traceability chain at the level of **research implications**. They are not Stage 13 command contracts, implemented examples or Stage 17 benchmark cases. No skill split is selected here.

| ID | Capability | Production responsibility and workflow implication | Decision artefact | Evaluation criterion | Future case seed, not an executed fixture |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C01 | Opportunity and market framing | Distinguish a customer/market hypothesis from an operating fact and discovery from execution. | Bounded opportunity with conditions and unknowns | Material market assumptions and alternatives are visible; no universal opportunity score | Attractive offer in an unsuitable or unverified market |
| C02 | Customer and problem evidence | Establish whose problem matters, who buys/uses and what was actually observed. | Customer/problem account with provenance | Declared segment, observed customer and unsuitable prospect remain distinct | Enthusiastic free users who are not the intended buyers |
| C03 | Value and alternatives | Relate desired progress, confidence, effort and delay to actual alternatives. | Value hypothesis and obstacle map | Claims trace to evidence or remain hypotheses; perception is not delivered value | Powerful promise with unproven customer benefit |
| C04 | Offer construction and obligations | Connect scope, components, deliverability, proof and remedies. | Coherent offer hypothesis | Every promise and added component has purpose, cost, scope and approval needs | Bonus-rich guarantee that cannot be fulfilled economically |
| C05 | Pricing and packaging | Compare price structure and real options under customer and economic constraints. | Price/packaging options | Total price and material terms are explicit; willingness to pay is not invented | Premium-price claim based only on a cheap initial test |
| C06 | Revenue and offer sequencing | Explain who pays, for what, when and why an additional offer belongs. | Revenue-sequence hypothesis | Each step creates value and has economic justification; four offer types are not mandatory | Multi-offer complexity before an entry offer works |
| C07 | Channel fit and bounded growth | Choose reach mechanisms appropriate to the audience and assess incremental expansion. | Channel hypothesis and scale/repair decision | Downstream economics, cash and capacity constrain volume | Cheap traffic from a channel with poor customer fit |
| C08 | Engagement and qualification | Distinguish contact, interest, suitability and progression through a commercial path. | Qualification and engagement model | A useful magnet or contactable lead is not automatically a customer or permission | High opt-ins but little qualified purchase intent |

| C10 | Sales path and conversion | Match buyer needs, alternatives, scope and acceptance to an appropriate buying process. | Commercial-path diagnosis or option brief | Suitable informed agreement is distinct from a forced yes; execution authority is external | Qualified buyers blocked by an unsuitable sales path |
| C11 | Delivery, capacity and quality | Connect promises to repeatable work, human limits, costs and learning time. | Delivery/capacity assessment | Actual fulfilment, workload and quality can support proposed demand | Growing sales overload a finite service team |
| C12 | Retention, repeat and renewal | Separate continued value and voluntary repeat purchase from continued billing. | Retention/renewal hypothesis | Matched cohorts, value delivery and exit behaviour are visible | Acquisition growth exactly offsets customer loss |

| C14 | Referrals and partnerships | Connect delivered value, incentives, partner fit and propagation mechanisms. | Referral/partner hypothesis | Suitable incremental customers, costs and counterparties are identified | Reward volume increases while referral quality falls |
| C15 | Unit economics and metric meaning | Define costs, margins, lifetime value and acquisition cost before interpreting them. | Reproducible economics assessment | Units, period, cost basis and assumptions agree; arithmetic passes | Revenue LTV compared to gross-profit LTV without reconciliation |
| C16 | Cash timing and exposure | Assess when usable cash arrives relative to commitments, refunds and future delivery. | Cash/payback scenario | Commitments and receivables are not cash; nominal profitability cannot override a cash gap | Annual prepayments fund growth but leave unfunded service obligations |
| C17 | Assumption identification | Surface the conditions on which value, demand, delivery and economics depend. | Decision-relevant assumption questions | Observation, belief, consequence and uncertainty stay separate | Confident business narrative omits its riskiest assumption |
| C18 | Experiment design | Start from a decision, choose adequate evidence, then minimise necessary effort. | Bounded experiment proposal | Results can change the decision; test fidelity, truthfulness and interpretation limits are explicit | Cheap interest poll used as proof of paid retention |
| C19 | Measurement and learning | Connect exposure and observations to interpretable, auditable learning. | Learning/evidence record | Denominators, cohorts, uncertainty and source records support the conclusion | Aggregate growth hides unchanged cohort performance |
| C20 | Constraint diagnosis and repair | Locate the responsible failure layer and choose no change, local correction or justified pivot. | Bounded change rationale | Evidence implicates changed decisions; unaffected accepted decisions are preserved | Channel failure triggers a needless entire-business rewrite |
| C21 | Business-system review and resilience | Judge coherence against actual owner objectives and plausible failure conditions. | Multidimensional business review | No single score or growth target replaces value, economics, cash and constraints | Sufficient business incorrectly rejected for not pursuing venture-scale growth |
| C22 | Human and specialist handoffs | Keep recommendations distinct from authorised execution and professional conclusions. | Bounded handoff with questions, facts and return evidence | Owner authority, operator roles and legal/accounting boundaries remain explicit | Availability of a tool mistaken for authority to spend or contact people |

## 7. Decisions, rejected alternatives and exit

**Retained:** a capability-shaped, source-traceable evidence base covering customer/value, offer, acquisition, sales, delivery, retention, economics, cash, experimentation and repair. **Merged:** duplicated responsibilities, while preserving conflicting metric meanings and context. **Adapted:** commercially useful heuristics to evidence, deliverability, truthful communication and human authority. **Rejected:** automatic growth imperatives, one universal score, concealed material terms, forced acceptance, book-shaped skills and the idea that repeated author anecdotes establish universal effectiveness.

Other rejected alternatives: use only short web summaries despite the supplied books; choose the most aggressive conflicting recommendation; replace source assertions silently with a cleaner framework; republish the PDFs or their proprietary examples; select skill counts now; implement speculative tools or benchmarks to make a research stage appear mature. The source matrices and this taxonomy are research outputs, not installed skills.

The Stage 1 owner/professional map, independent-installation requirement, separate quality dimensions, human approvals and initial non-goals remain unchanged. No new owner-specific business objective, budget or live action is assumed. No additional user decision is required to complete this source-extraction stage.

**Exit assessment:** All five books have been converted into attributed evidence and critically reconciled candidate responsibilities rather than five parallel playbooks. Required broader-validation questions remain explicit. Stage completion still requires the actual checks and remote commit receipt in [conformance](2026-09-09-stage-02-conformance.md) and [bootstrap progress](bootstrap-2-progress.md). Stage 3 must reconstruct its task from the original specification and these accepted records; no Stage 3 output is claimed here.
