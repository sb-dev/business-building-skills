## 3. Coverage-driven comparisons and depth

The first level decides one bounded question; it does not require rebuilding every upstream artefact. Level two creates one coherent commercial component with its input/output and obligation links. Level three covers every business-map concern for one small exchange and a feasible next experiment. Level four begins with accepted versions and actual fictional faults, compares rival explanations and repairs only implicated layers. Level five assembles a complete business thesis, alternative choices, binding constraints and a sequenced evidence/commitment programme with dependencies, rather than producing a longer Level 3 canvas.

The selected local service replaces an abstract solo-service example with geographic and full-cycle work. The ecommerce example supplies physical stock and reverse-flow exposure instead of another digital product. All three original level-five classes are retained because consumer subscriptions, finite high-ticket services and contributor-based open-source commerce create different structural tests. The primary set need not implement every pack; no original §22 requirement imposes that quota.

Some cases deliberately share coverage labels. The differentiator is their task depth and failure mechanism, not merely unique tags. Renewal comprehension is integrated into E06; specialised SaaS packaging into E08; digital rights into E03/E15; and full advisory practice into E14. These are substantive consolidation decisions. Unselected cases remain genuine alternatives, not a backlog promised to satisfy a missing current requirement.
