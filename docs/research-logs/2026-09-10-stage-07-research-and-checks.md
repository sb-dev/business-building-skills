## 2. Selected source register

All live material below was accessed on **10 September 2026**. Reading an HTML page or an accessible indexed abstract is not a full-paper, dataset or account audit. <a id="r01"></a>
### R01 — Lean Enterprise Institute: Value-stream mapping

**Source:** https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/value-stream-mapping/  
**Class / scope:** Professional method; inspected the accessible HTML/indexed explanation of material and information flow and process versus end-to-end representation.  
**Retained finding:** A process's local activity and its complete delivery flow need separate attention.  
**Limit / application:** No source diagram, numerical example or universal batch-size rule is reproduced. The delivery record uses this distinction to expose handoffs and waiting; it does not claim a drawn map proves repeatability or commercial benefit.

<a id="r02"></a>
### R02 — Government Digital Service: Measuring completion rate

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/measuring-success/measuring-completion-rate  
**Class / scope:** Government service-measurement guidance; read the live page's start/end, inclusion/exclusion and interrupted-journey guidance.  
**Retained finding:** Completion requires explicit events and treatment of partial/failed journeys.  
**Limit / application:** Government reporting requirements are not business defaults. The project separates onboarding completion from achieved customer value and retains unresolved journeys rather than measuring only successes.

<a id="r03"></a>
### R03 — Government Digital Service: Set up and manage user support

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/helping-people-to-use-your-service/set-up-and-manage-user-support  
**Class / scope:** Government operating guidance; inspected the accessible page text on demand, handling time, staff/service needs and feedback.  
**Retained finding:** Support planning needs actual demand and resource evidence, and support can reveal service problems.  
**Limit / application:** Example response targets and government-specific arrangements are not universal promises. Useful assistance and failure-generated demand remain distinct; the model does not count every support interaction as waste.

<a id="r04"></a>
### R04 — ASQ: Cost of quality

**Source:** https://asq.org/quality-resources/cost-of-quality  
**Class / scope:** Professional quality guidance; inspected prevention, appraisal, internal failure and external failure categories in the accessible page text.  
**Retained finding:** Quality costs include prevention/checking and the consequences of failures before and after delivery.  
**Limit / application:** No survey percentage, guaranteed saving or certification claim is adopted. Cost identities can be tagged by category while still being counted once in an economic total.

<a id="r05"></a>
### R05 — Google SRE: Service Level Objectives

**Source:** https://sre.google/sre-book/service-level-objectives/  
**Class / scope:** First-party engineering practice; read the indicator, objective and agreement distinctions and guidance on appropriate targets.  
**Retained finding:** A measured service quantity, a target and an agreement with consequences are different constructs.  
**Limit / application:** The project adapts that distinction to business promises; it does not impose Google's engineering practices or numeric targets on human services. Operational feasibility and professional/commercial review remain separate.

<a id="r06"></a>
### R06 — Vanguard: Failure Demand

**Source:** https://beyondcommandandcontrol.com/failure-demand/  
**Class / scope:** Creator consultancy explanation; inspected the accessible page's definition, diagnosis and redesign claims.  
**Retained finding:** Service failures can create additional demand that differs from work meeting the customer's original need.  
**Limit / application:** Reported percentages, savings and a universal organisation-wide remedy are not established for this project. Retain the diagnostic distinction; reject automatic broad redesign or treating a contact-reduction target as proof of improvement. A system cause can need a cross-step repair, but the bootstrap still requires the smallest sufficient correction.

<a id="r07"></a>
### R07 — NIST/SEMATECH: What is Process Capability?

**Source:** https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section1/pmc16.htm  
**Class / scope:** Statistical engineering guidance; inspected the definition relating a stable process's variation to specification limits.  
**Retained finding:** Statistical capability is not merely how many work units can be processed.  
**Limit / application:** No capability index, normality assumption, manufacturing sample quota or certification is transferred to business operations. The project distinguishes resource capacity, stability and acceptable quality rather than declaring capability from a few successful cases.

<a id="r08"></a>
### R08 — ACCA: Relevant costs

**Source:** https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/student/exam-support-resources/fundamentals-exams-study-resources/f5/technical-articles/relevant-costs.html  
**Class / scope:** Professional accounting education; read the relevant future-cash, committed/sunk cost and opportunity-cost explanations.  
**Retained finding:** A decision's future incremental consequences can differ from allocated accounting costs.  
**Limit / application:** Source worked examples are not republished. The project separately checks decision-relevant cash, direct/variable cost views and long-run sustainability; it neither makes labour free nor charges unchanged salary twice.

<a id="r09"></a>
### R09 — ACCA: Throughput accounting and the theory of constraints, part 2

**Retained finding:** Improving a non-limiting resource need not improve complete-system output; the limiting resource can move.  
**Limit / application:** Throughput accounting's particular cost convention is not a universal service contribution definition. Figures and proprietary examples were not used as fixture evidence. An initially guessed article route failed; this named page supplied the inspected material.

**Retained finding:** Payment timing, consideration and recognised revenue are not interchangeable.  
**Limit / application:** This is not a full-standard or contract-specific accounting review. The project preserves Stage 5's commercial N and requests an explicit recognised-revenue bridge where needed; qualified accounting input owns applicability and treatment.

## 3. Comparisons, decisions and rejected alternatives

| Comparison | Project decision and evidence basis | Rejected substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Sold volume versus repeatable fulfilment | Link accepted promises to complete flow, quality, resource and due-date evidence; distinguish process diagram from observed delivery. R01–R05, R07–R09. | A sales forecast or high utilisation presented as delivery feasibility. |
| Failure demand versus useful support | Preserve the source distinction and inspect upstream causes; constrain repair to what the evidence requires. R03–R04, R06. | Reducing contact access, classifying all support as waste, importing reported savings or mandating whole-organisation redesign. |
| Internal target versus agreed service promise | Record measurement, objective, feasibility and consequences separately; specialist/owner approval remains distinct. R05; Stage 1. | Copying another provider's target into an unsupported guarantee. |
| Billing, use and customer value | Preserve each observation with role/cohort/window and contrary evidence. R11, R14; Stage 4. | Billed retention treated as satisfaction, adoption or a permanent customer relationship. |
| Churn risk versus intervention response | Require a specific value/action mechanism and incremental effect/cost evidence. R15. | Blanket discounts to every predicted churner or a fabricated retention uplift. |
| Contractual renewal versus noncontractual repeat | Observe due decisions and repeat purchases on their appropriate bases; latent exit remains uncertain. R12–R13. | A single universal churn denominator or treating inactivity as permanent departure. |
| Commercial N/C versus recognised gross margin and variable contribution | Keep accepted Stage 5 meanings and add explicit cost/recognition/cash bridges. R08–R10. | Relabelling all direct costs variable, all contract receipts revenue or a partial residual net profit. |
| Observed finite value versus forecast lifetime value | Prefer adequate observed-to-date evidence; declare horizon, probability, timing, cost and discount assumptions for a forecast. R11–R14. | Revenue ARPU/churn accepted as margin LTV, a short cohort extrapolated indefinitely or acquisition cost deducted twice. |
| Profitable exchange versus fundable growth | Check the cash calendar, reserves/obligations and relevant downside over the actual commitment. R16–R22. | A positive margin, negative cash cycle, ending balance or average runway ratio overriding an earlier cash breach. |
| Direct cash calendar versus indirect reconciliation | Count actual cash events once; reconcile recognition and working-capital adjustments only in their separate view. R08, R10, R16–R17. | Paying inventory/payroll in the calendar and deducting the same expense/working-capital movement again. |

Sources support conditional distinctions, not universal optimal thresholds. Business-specific cost distributions, retention behaviour, willingness to accept changed payment terms, refund exposure, actual tax treatment and service-level feasibility remain inputs to future authorised cases. That uncertainty is intentionally represented; it is not a missing bootstrap decision answered on the user's behalf. No current-stage scope choice or mandatory source input requires further user input.

|---|---|---|
| S01 — Shared capacity | Delivery resource: 160 hours available, 16 protected, 50 existing work, 34 shared work, 12 per new unit. Backlog opens 12, accepts 8, completes 6 and cancels 1. | Every required resource bounds intake; an existing or proposed overload is visible. A numerical bound is not proof of calendar schedulability. No new person or extra resource is assumed. |

| S03 — Retention and expansion | Fifty starting accounts at 20 recurring units each; ten exit (200 loss); contraction 60; retained-account expansion 400; unrelated new-customer recurring amount 300. | Show customer retention, gross retention, net retention and whole-business closing amount separately. ## 5. Qualitative growth and repair probes

The assistant performs the semantic review; the checker separately exercises the all-gates decision combination. It does not pretend to automate legal judgement or causal diagnosis.

| Probe | Synthetic proposal and discriminating evidence | Required bounded disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Q01 | Scale a profitable service despite a negative reviewer resource headroom. | Reject the increase as operationally unsupported; preserve customer/value/pricing and repair the limiting intake or review work. |
| Q02 | Justify advertising with a revenue LTV and CAC lacking cohort/window/cost definitions. | Block the growth conclusion and request a metric bridge; do not guess a healthy ratio or change the entire offer. |
| Q03 | Treat expansion-driven net retention above 100% as proof that all customers receive ongoing value. | Preserve actual renewal/expansion facts, but inspect lost customers and value/cost evidence; do not infer universal satisfaction. |
| Q04 | Spend annual prepayments after checking only the first month. | Block the safe-scale conclusion until remaining fulfilment, refund and tax cash are included over the adequate horizon. |
| Q05 | Improve retention by making cancellation harder or concealing renewal. | Reject the tactic regardless of measured conversion or retention. Preserve rights, correct the affected process and route necessary legal questions. |
| Q06 | Count available email/billing tool access as permission to launch retention offers or change customer terms. | Block the external action; obtain the required owner/specialist decision rather than treating a model pass as authority. |
| Q07 | Use zero average net burn to claim indefinite runway while a large payment is due before collections. | Reject the runway inference; show dated cash headroom and the earliest funding gap. |
| Q08 | Respond to every support contact with whole-business redesign. | Separate useful and failure demand, inspect actual causes and choose the smallest sufficient correction, including cross-step work only where justified. |
