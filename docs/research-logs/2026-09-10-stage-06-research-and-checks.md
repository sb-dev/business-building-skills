## 2. Primary-source register

### R01: HubSpot, Use contact and company lifecycle stages

[Primary documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/use-lifecycle-stages). Overview, default/customisable stages and lifecycle update/time-property descriptions inspected. The later fetch showed an update date of 17 July 2026. **Finding:** workflow labels depend on defined events and configuration. **Use:** keep event evidence, account/contact units and timing distinct. **Limit:** a provider's default customer or qualified-lead label is not this project's universal business definition, and configuration is not customer validation.

### R02: Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava and Chapsky, Advertising measurement

[Publisher abstract](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mksc.2018.1135), *A Comparison of Approaches to Advertising Measurement: Evidence from Big Field Experiments at Facebook*, Marketing Science, 2019. Abstract and publication metadata read; full paper, appendix and data not read. **Finding:** observational methods often disagreed with experimental effects in the studied campaigns despite rich controls. **Use:** distinguish attributed outcomes from causal incremental outcomes. **Limit:** no local effect size or universal claim that advertising is ineffective follows.

### R03: Blake, Nosko and Tadelis, Paid-search heterogeneity

[NBER abstract and published-version record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20171); [publisher abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA12423). *Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness: A Large-Scale Field Experiment*, working paper 2014, Econometrica 2015. Official indexed abstracts/metadata retrieved; page opens also returned errors. **Finding:** the eBay experiments differed by keyword/customer context and challenged naive observational returns. **Use:** compare segment and existing-demand effects, not just clicks. **Limit:** this is one underlying study, not two corroborating studies or a universal paid-search result. No full-paper reading or imported rate is claimed.

### R04: Schmitt, Skiera and Van den Bulte, Referral Programs and Customer Value

[Publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.1509/jm.75.1.46), Journal of Marketing, 2011. Abstract and metadata read. **Finding:** referred customers' observed value and retention differed in the studied bank, with differences across segments and over time. **Use:** inspect actual referred-customer quality rather than reward count. **Limit:** the reported context does not establish a universal incremental programme effect; no reported uplift is used as a project forecast or customer-value multiplier.

### R05: ICO, Business-to-business marketing

[Official guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/). Overview, personal-data/electronic-marketing distinctions and relevant contact considerations inspected. **Finding:** subscriber, medium, purpose and personal-data context affect the review. The page explicitly says it is under review after Data (Use and Access) Act changes. **Use:** retain context and route permission questions. **Limit:** UK guidance, not a universal legal classifier, automatic consent exception or approval to use public contact details. No detailed legal conclusion is supplied by this stage.

### R06: Google Search, Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content

[Primary guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content). Relevant content-purpose and search guidance read. **Finding:** useful material and discoverability are related but different concerns. **Use:** connect content to actual audience needs and measure downstream relevance. **Limit:** no ranking guarantee, production-volume rule or proof that content is the best acquisition channel; search guidance does not validate the underlying offer.

### R07: Open Source Guides, Finding Users for Your Project

[Primary practitioner guide](https://opensource.guide/finding-users/). Audience, explanation, discovery and participation sections read. **Finding:** relevant users need to understand and find a useful project. **Use:** conditional content/community/distribution hypotheses in developer-facing contexts. **Limit:** practical guidance is not an empirical commercial-effect estimate. Historical platform examples and quoted third-party experiences are not reproduced or treated as current channel recommendations.

### R08: Open Source Guides, Building Welcoming Communities

[Primary practitioner guide](https://opensource.guide/building-community/). Welcoming participation, documentation, responsiveness, gathering places and community-growth responsibilities inspected. **Finding:** community participation brings continuing responsibilities, not just a pool of prospects. **Use:** expose support/moderation effort and keep contribution distinct from purchasing. **Limit:** not a causal revenue study or permission to publish sensitive incidents or contact members commercially.

### R09: Apple Developer, Creating your product page

[Primary documentation](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/product-page/). Discovery/metadata, description, in-app purchase and product-page optimisation sections inspected. **Finding:** platform discovery and evaluation have their own surfaces and controls. **Use:** model platform eligibility, truthful presentation and observable adoption separately. **Limit:** no guaranteed placement, download-to-paid rate, future platform requirement or claim that a listed product has customer demand. No platform action or experiment was executed.

[Primary documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/private-offers-for-channel). Definitions and alternative deal-structure descriptions read. **Finding:** partner, supplier, customer and billing responsibilities depend on the actual arrangement. **Use:** make partner-led/marketplace-mediated roles and handoffs explicit. **Limit:** descriptive provider rules, not evidence that an enterprise buyer prefers this path. No fee rate, geographic eligibility or agreement is adopted as a universal input.

[Primary practitioner explanation](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/product-management/product-led-growth). Main definition, journey and sales/qualification discussion read, not unrelated navigation material. **Finding:** product experience can participate in acquisition and coexist with assisted sales. **Use:** inspect meaningful use, paid transition and user/buyer differences. **Limit:** provider promotional claims about effectiveness or high conversion are not independent empirical evidence. A tactic may solve different tasks in different settings: an event can primarily support a complex sales relationship rather than immediate checkout; a marketplace can simplify procurement without creating new demand; product use can substantiate value without identifying the payer. The architecture therefore composes paths rather than imposing a single funnel. These are reasoned design interpretations, not additional measured empirical results.

Rejected designs: one universal channel winner; forcing every business through all classes; substituting warm/cold outreach for the original separate affiliates/partnerships classes; forcing every user into a contact record; treating all path labels as mutually exclusive; a weighted lead score that masks missing authority/rights; attributed revenue as causal profit; and a lower-cost lead metric detached from sales workload and retained economics. All original categories remain intact.

## 4. Completed synthetic channel-selection decisions

These decisions apply the researched framework to **invented** contexts. Their inputs are neither observations about the user nor empirical market claims. They test whether the model yields bounded, intelligible decisions, not whether a channel works in the market. No campaign, message or expenditure was executed. Each fictional owner acts only within the stated fixture.

### CS01: capacity-limited professional service

**Context and objective:** A fictional service has a ceiling of six deliveries per comparable period and wants positive direct contribution after acquisition, not maximal lead volume. The accepted offer and target are unchanged. Synthetic mature records in S01 compare paid-media responses with partner introductions; both have the same declared CU600 fully valued acquisition cost. The partner path generates more suitable paid customers at lower effort, under these invented observations.

**Candidates and evidence:** Retain the offer; compare CH05 paid media, CH06 partnerships and a bounded CH01 follow-up to suitable authorised enquiries. Research R02/R10 establishes measurement/role questions, not the fixture's conversion rates. Existing-audience access and referrals are unproved; content/search/community/events would require additional effort/time not evidenced here; marketplace/platform/product-led/affiliate mechanisms have no demonstrated dependency that justifies adding them for this decision.

**Decision:** Do not expand the cheaper-per-lead paid-media option on that metric alone. Prefer a bounded test of the partner mechanism's repeatability within remaining delivery capacity, subject to verified buyer/partner authority and funded costs. S01's economics support comparison within the fixture, not a market forecast.

**Action boundary and review:** The fictional owner may authorise only a separately agreed small test after confirming the partner's role, contact/data permissions and fulfilment slots; this record is not that authorisation. Stop/review if suitable demand exceeds capacity, costs or quality depart from the assumptions, or the partner cannot make truthful commitments. Preserve the accepted customer, offer and existing obligations.

### CS02: product experience without paid-buyer proof

**Context and objective:** A fictional developer product has 40 trial accounts, of which 20 performed a defined useful task; four asked about team use. No payment or budget authority is recorded. Current trial support consumes eight of ten available support hours per week. The objective is to determine a feasible paid complement, not maximise signups.

**Candidates and evidence:** CH12 product-led acquisition plus CH02 documentation/content and bounded SP02 sales assistance can investigate actual users' needs and payer roles. R07/R11 provides the mechanism; Stage 4/5 prevents use from becoming paid demand. Paid media, paid affiliates and broad platform rollout would expand an unproved and nearly constrained system; enterprise/marketplace expansion has no validated procurement need. Community support is useful context, not a prospect list; cold outreach, search investment, events and unrelated partnerships are not shown to resolve the immediate gap.

**Decision:** Investigate the four voluntary team-use enquiries through an appropriately authorised buying/value conversation and preserve a self-serve option. Do not mark them paid opportunities solely from usage, change the free entitlement or buy growth to compensate for missing evidence.

**Action boundary and review:** A later owner-approved test must expose paid scope/price and actual payer authority, support burden and genuine buying actions. Reassess when those claims have evidence; block expansion that would breach the support limit. No universal product-qualified-lead threshold or conversion rate is inferred from the four enquiries.

### CS03: enterprise procurement, not a reach deficit

**Context and objective:** A fictional existing supplier has two interested enterprise accounts. The actual users are supportive, but one buyer says procurement requires a specified marketplace route. Partner resale authority, listing readiness, budget approval and payment dates are unresolved. The objective is to understand the lawful and operationally feasible purchase path.

**Candidates and evidence:** Compare direct SP04 enterprise completion, SP07 marketplace-mediated and SP08 partner-led paths using CH06/CH10/CH11. R10/R16 demonstrates that these arrangements have different prerequisites; it does not prove the user's requested route is available. More paid/search/content/event/referral/affiliate/community/PLG reach does not resolve the present approval dependency. Existing-audience/direct communication can clarify the actual buyer requirement only within authority.

**Decision:** Block a promise that the business is marketplace-ready. Verify the buyer's requirement, supplier/partner rights, exact transaction roles, delivery and collection obligations, then compare the simpler permitted route. Preserve the accepted offer unless the verified procurement requirements imply a substantive change.

**Action boundary and review:** No platform enrolment, agreement acceptance, private offer, data transfer or commitment is authorised by this design. Review after the named buyer and relevant owner/specialists resolve prerequisites; lack of evidence is not proof that the channel is impossible or that the enterprise segment is invalid.

## 5. Synthetic fixtures specified before execution

All quantities, prices, rates and businesses below are original **synthetic** inputs. Currency is CU. Cost exclusions are explicit; no partial residual is labelled net profit. The deterministic checker will test arithmetic and enumerations, while manual review tests the scope and repair. These are not the fifteen primary progressive examples, a later stress-test suite or installed-skill benchmarks.

### S01: cheap leads versus suitable acquired customers

Over equally mature comparable periods, channel A has 100 distinct leads, 10 qualified opportunities and two paid new customers. Cash acquisition spend is CU200, with ten hours valued at CU40/hour. Channel B has 40 distinct leads, 20 qualified opportunities and four paid new customers; cash spend CU400 and five hours valued at CU40/hour. The valuation includes the acquisition/sales work for all leads; those costs are not in delivery contribution. Direct contribution before acquisition is CU200 per acquired customer. Shared overhead, tax and later periods are excluded, and no further refunds/costs are assumed within this fixture.

Expected: cash cost per lead A=CU2, B=CU10; fully valued A=CU600 for each channel; full cost per qualified opportunity CU60 versus CU30; attributed full cost per paid customer CU300 versus CU150; acquisition-adjusted contribution CU−200 versus CU200. Qualification is 10% versus 50%, while qualified-to-paid conversion is 20% for both. Compare quality and cost basis before choosing the superficially cheaper lead. Six total customers use six delivery slots; do not promise unlimited volume at these averages.

### S02: a changed mix is not proof of a weaker offer

Synthetic population one: 90 high-intent and ten low-intent eligible prospects, with 36 and one observed purchases. Population two: ten high-intent and 90 low-intent prospects, with four and nine purchases. Within-group conversion is 40% and 10% in both populations; aggregate conversion changes from 37% to 13%.

Expected disposition: inspect the changed acquisition mix and relevant qualification definition, preserving the offer and sales process unless separate evidence implicates them. These fixed synthetic outcomes demonstrate arithmetic composition, not a causal experiment or statistically estimated treatment effect.

### S03: immature opportunities and time-to-close

Synthetic cohort of ten opportunities starts on day 0. At day 10, two are won, one lost and seven open. At day 30, five are won, two lost and three open. Won durations at day 30 are 4, 10, 14, 20 and 24 days; the three still-open opportunities are each at age 30, not zero duration.

Expected: observed cohort win proportions 20% at day 10 and 50% at day 30; closed-only win rates are 2/3 and 5/7 and must disclose their restricted denominators. The closed-won mean is 14.4 days and median 14, neither a prediction of the open opportunities' final durations. Do not compare day-10 and day-30 ratios as equal-maturity channel performance or declare open work lost.

### S04: identity and attribution reconciliation

Synthetic records contain five contact IDs at two distinct accounts and two account-level opportunities. Three unique paid transactions total CU100, CU80 and CU120; one account makes two purchases. Transactions have overlapping source labels: content/search, affiliate/referral and partner/marketplace. Whole-credit platform reports would sum to CU600, but actual canonical transaction consideration is CU300. Six source-label observations do not create six transactions.

Expected disposition: preserve five contacts, two accounts, two opportunities and three transactions as different units; reconcile total consideration once. Under an explicitly chosen equal fractional credit illustration, six tag entries total CU300, not CU600. This allocation is bookkeeping, not evidence that each tag caused half the outcome. Unknown identity is not repaired through covert tracking.

### S05: low acquisition cost, weak downstream result

Synthetic channels X and Y each acquire ten paid customers for CU500 acquisition cost. Each charges CU100/customer, with CU500 delivery cost in the cohort. By the common observation point X refunds CU400 and incurs CU200 additional support, while Y refunds CU100 and incurs CU100 support. No other direct or shared costs, fees, taxes or later revenue are included.

Expected: both show CU50 acquisition cost per paid customer. X has N=CU600, K=CU700, C=CU−100 and C−A=CU−600. Y has N=CU900, K=CU600, C=CU300 and C−A=CU−200. Y is less negative but neither supports profitable acquisition scale on these facts. The next action addresses evidenced customer fit/delivery/refund economics, not a confident growth recommendation based on equal CAC or hoped-for lifetime value.

### S06: delivery and cash gates survive attractive acquisition

Synthetic 12 available leads are all qualified and would, by assumption, buy the same service at CU1,000 each, paid day 45. Four existing commitments already occupy four of ten total delivery slots. Each further customer needs one slot and CU600 delivery cash on day 14. After funding the existing obligations, only CU2,500 is available for new work. No deposit, borrowing, fee or other cost is assumed.

Expected: the remaining capacity could serve six additional customers, but cash funds only four. The immediate responsible limit under these assumptions is four, not 12 or six, leaving CU100 before eventual collection. This is not a real conversion forecast or recommendation to borrow. Preserve the evidenced offer and investigate approved timing/capacity options rather than increasing lead volume.

### S07: same symptom, different repair

Five independent synthetic records specify: (a) intended customers cannot access the published page because the link fails; (b) giveaway entrants request only an unrelated free reward; (c) suitable users interpret a paid service as a free tool due to inaccurate wording; (d) an informed suitable buyer rejects a genuinely missing required capability; (e) a willing buyer encounters a reproduced payment error under an otherwise unchanged accepted offer.

Expected dispositions: (a) repair access and retest reach; (b) review incentive/qualification; (c) correct the message and preserve the real offer; (d) route the missing capability/value constraint to offer review without inventing it; (e) fix the conversion execution defect without automatic repricing. A symptom is not a universal funnel diagnosis. Other real cases could remain inconclusive; this fixture supplies the specific local evidence.

### S08: authority and evidence gates cannot be averaged away

Five synthetic proposals attempt to: (a) treat automatic email opens as purchase intent; (b) promote a lead with unknown buyer authority to an approved paid opportunity based on a high engagement score; (c) send repeated promotion after an explicit opt-out; (d) present an affiliate's paid endorsement as independent; and (e) claim a listed product's prospective sales pipeline is collected cash.

Expected dispositions: (a) retain the measurement limitation, not a qualified-demand claim; (b) seek the missing authority evidence without declaring inability to buy; (c) block the disallowed action and respect the opt-out; (d) block the misleading presentation and obtain proper review; (e) preserve listing/prospect/commitment/payment states and reconcile actual cash. No enforcement evasion, legal certification or live outreach occurs.
