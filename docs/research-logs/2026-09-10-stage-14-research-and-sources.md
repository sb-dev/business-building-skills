## 2. Source register

### R01 — Stripe: usage-based billing

Source: https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/how-it-works

Read the live overview, usage lifecycle and Billing Meters concepts. Meter ingestion, aggregation, price configuration and billing are distinct operations; current guidance also differentiates existing basic billing from a newer integration recommendation. Retain the need to reconcile consumed, billable and invoiced units. Do not adopt a required provider, migration, price, metering implementation or claim that billed usage proves customer value. The live page differed from an older indexed snippet; the live text was used. No account or meter was tested.

### R02 — Apple: offer auto-renewable subscriptions

Source: https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/offer-auto-renewable-subscriptions

Read the substantive subscription-group, product, duration, renewal and billing-recovery guidance after the navigation. These establish platform-managed commercial and entitlement distinctions. Proceeds depend on applicable conditions rather than a single universal fee. Retain store/territory/terms/cohort as evidence context, not hard-coded commissions, guaranteed renewals or a mandate to use one purchase channel. No StoreKit integration, terms review for a client or App Store account was exercised.

### R03 — Google Play: subscriptions

Source: https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/subscriptions

Read base plans, offers, prepaid versus auto-renewing arrangements, instalment commitments and management/cancellation distinctions. Commitment period, payment frequency and entitlement are different facts. Retain these questions in the mobile grammar; do not assume every subscription auto-renews or every annual commitment is paid upfront. Platform code, regional availability and refund entitlements were not imported as universal rules. No billing code was run.

### R04 — Xero: professional services accounting

Source: https://www.xero.com/uk/guides/professional-services-accounting/

The guide is dated 10 March 2026. Read its billing-method, project/time and cost discussion: time-based, fixed-fee, milestone and retainer arrangements have different tracking needs. Use that comparison to distinguish sold scope, effort, acceptance, invoicing and cash. Reject the implication that a fixed fee guarantees the amount and date actually collected. No tax, recognition, employment-status or universal utilisation advice is adopted; no client accounts were inspected.

### R05 — Management Consultancies Association: Consulting Excellence

Source: https://www.mca.org.uk/value-of-consulting/consulting-excellence

Read the framework's client-value, transparency, ethical and professional-development commitments. These are professional norms, not empirical proof that a consultancy creates a given return. They support explicit client outcomes and delivery quality in an advisory mode; they do not establish a separate execution platform, certification of this project or permission to guarantee business outcomes.

### R06 — Management Consultancies Association: value of consulting

Source: https://www.mca.org.uk/value-of-consulting

Read the association's account of expertise, client outcomes, accountability and knowledge/skills transfer. Use it to distinguish delivery of advice from client adoption and realised impact. The association's advocacy is not independent causal measurement. The project conclusion is that these distinctions fit a services advisory mode, rather than requiring another nearly identical core pack.

### R07 — Cabinet Office: Consultancy Playbook publication

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-consultancy-playbook

Read the HTML publication metadata and description, published 5 September 2022, concerning commissioning, value and knowledge transfer. The linked playbook and guidance PDFs were **not read**. This is limited support for the existence of client-side commissioning/transfer responsibilities, not a claim to have applied the complete playbook or government procurement rules to every consultant.

### R08 — Shopify: inventory reports

Source: https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/reports-and-analytics/shopify-reports/report-types/default-reports/inventory-reports

Read inventory-state and measurement definitions, including sold quantities, available/committed/incoming stock, report windows and zero-denominator behaviour. Provider sell-through and days-remaining calculations use particular bases and limitations; revenue-based ABC categories do not include cost. Retain physical quantity, valuation, cash and cohort distinctions. Do not turn a sales grade or reported days remaining into an automatic purchase decision. No report, inventory adjustment or forecasting model was executed.

### R09 — Sharetribe: marketplace liquidity

Source: https://www.sharetribe.com/marketplace-glossary/liquidity/

Read the February 2024 article's two-sided matching and metric sections. It distinguishes buyer and seller outcomes, but its purchase/search rate prose inverts the fraction while describing a share of visits or requests leading to transactions. Record this discrepancy; do not copy the inverted formula. The project uses a defined matched-request numerator over eligible requests and retains unfilled requests. Reject universal rate targets, automatic network effects and one-metric approval. The local numeric check is independent arithmetic, not a marketplace performance test.

### R10 — Stripe Connect: charge types

Source: https://docs.stripe.com/connect/charges

Read the comparison of charge/funds flows and refund/dispute responsibility. The operational arrangement changes whose balance is affected. Retain own fees, third-party funds, liabilities and timing separately; a funds-flow option does not itself settle accounting principal/agent treatment or legal responsibility. No processor is selected and no money was moved.

### R11 — Lemon Squeezy: products

Source: https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/products

Read the product, variant, payment-type and linked file-version concepts. Digital downloads and recurring access can be distinct offers. Use this narrow capability evidence, not its navigation as proof that all linked capabilities were audited. No digital asset, account or product was created; no merchant-of-record or tax conclusion is inferred.

### R12 — Lemon Squeezy: generating licence keys

Source: https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/licensing/generating-license-keys

Read the distinction between non-subscription duration/activation settings and subscription-linked keys. Access enforcement is not the same as the contractual scope of a licence, support or update promise. Retain entitlement/version/support questions; do not require licence keys for every creative work, reproduce worked examples or let a technical revocation capability override existing customer rights.

### R13 — Patreon: selling one-time purchases

Source: https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-gb/articles/16303719836813-Selling-one-time-purchases-on-Patreon

Read the page updated 28 July 2026, particularly product/post/collection sales, membership overlap, after-purchase access, removal and payout distinctions. Access, sale, membership and settled funds are not the same event. Its product-removal/refund wording describes a provider surface, not a legal permission to withdraw promised access. Fee figures, country eligibility and payout durations remain task-specific checks, not pack defaults. No shop operation was executed.

### R14 — Open Source Guides: getting paid for open-source work

Source: https://opensource.guide/getting-paid/

Read the distinction between funding personal work and funding a project, and the discussion of support, sponsorship, grants, services and community/funder boundaries. Retain separate beneficiaries, payers and obligations. Historical examples are not verified current businesses and do not prove a universal monetisation sequence. The pack must protect contributor/community incentives rather than equate repository attention with paid demand.

### R15 — Open Source Initiative: Open Source Definition

Source: https://opensource.org/osd

Read the definition and criteria, including redistribution, modifications and non-discrimination by field of endeavour. Publicly viewable source alone does not establish an open-source licence. The research uses these distinctions without deciding the legal interpretation of a particular licence or authorising relicensing. Software rights, trademarks, hosted access and separately sold services retain their own reviewed boundaries.

### R16 — Google Business Profile: service areas

Source: https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en

Read service-area versus hybrid-business definitions and geographic/address configuration. This supports distinguishing the actual service footprint from a marketing label. The model's travel, appointment and resource checks are project operational reasoning, not claims that a listing proves delivery capacity. Provider limits and travel recommendations are not universal operating thresholds; no profile was read or changed.

### R17 — Agent Skills specification

Source: https://agentskills.io/specification

Read frontmatter, compatibility, body/resources and progressive disclosure. These support portable, local instructions and selective loading. The specification does **not** establish the business Extension Pack format defined here; that is a project contract. Experimental tool metadata does not grant authority. No host, installer or official validator was executed by reading the page.

## 3. Retrieval limitations and rejected extrapolations

Two Gumroad membership pages returned no readable live body. Their indexed snippets were discovery leads, not relied upon for detailed current behaviour. Readable Lemon Squeezy and Patreon documentation supplied the required digital-product distinctions instead. The Shopify returns landing page supplied only limited navigational content; no detailed return workflow is attributed to it. The catalogue's refund and rights constraints come from accepted core contracts, not an invented full-page read.

Three source challenges affect design. R09's rate inversion is explicitly rejected and tested with a finite original fixture. R04's fixed-fee certainty does not remove collection risk. R13's ability to delete paid content does not grant permission to negate agreed access. These are bounded distinctions between a source's words, tool capabilities and the project's accepted constraints, not blanket rejection of the source.

## 4. Actual candidate comparisons and decisions

| Comparison | Finding and decision | What would change the decision |
|---|---|---|
| Core only versus a pack for every project | Core can answer all general business questions. A pack qualifies only when reusable mechanism-specific prompts, metrics and failure checks add value without weakening the core. | Evidence that core already supplies the full specialised contract can remove redundant pack work. |
| `professional-services` versus `consulting-business` | Both price bounded human expertise with finite delivery and client dependencies. Retain professional-services with an explicit advisory/knowledge-transfer mode; merge the consulting seed into that mode. R04–R07. | Distinct repeated advisory needs that cannot be represented without incompatible assumptions would justify reconsidering an independent pack. A job title alone would not. |
| SaaS versus consumer mobile subscriptions | Retain separate grammars: recurring hosted-service economics versus store-mediated offer, billing and entitlement reconciliation. R01–R03. | A direct-only mobile service without material store-mediated mechanics can use SaaS/core; a mobile label alone does not trigger the second grammar. |
| Inventory ecommerce versus creator digital product | Retain distinct stock/fulfilment/reverse-flow and rights/access/version/production-cost grammars. R08, R11–R13. | Hybrid physical/digital bundles need explicit component scopes and shared-cost reconciliation, not a silent universal merge. |
| Marketplace versus selling one's own stock | Retain multi-sided matching, counterparty/funds and side-specific quality checks. R09–R10. A web shop is not automatically a marketplace. | A business that owns all stock and sells directly may need ecommerce instead; actual intermediary roles determine fit. |
| Open-source commercialisation versus ordinary SaaS | Retain rights, contributors, free adoption, funders and maintainers as separate actors and obligations. R14–R15. | A purely hosted proprietary product with no open-source ecosystem need can use SaaS; public code alone is insufficient. |
| Local service versus a location tag | Retain appointment, travel and geographic resource constraints. R16 plus core capacity rules. | A location-independent consultant working in London remains a services case; city wording creates no extra grammar. |
| Inheritance tree versus bounded composition | Select self-contained packs and explicit per-component scope. Do not introduce runtime inheritance or last-loaded-wins merging. | Repeated production evidence may justify a narrow shared resource under the family extraction rule; no such implementation evidence is claimed here. |

The selected research catalogue therefore contains **eight independent candidate grammars and one merged candidate mode**, covering all nine original seeds. These are design dispositions, not eight implemented or validated packs. No user-owned business decision remains unresolved. Actual client facts, permissions and jurisdiction remain required inputs to future work, not gaps filled by a pack default.
