## 1. Comparison discipline

The Stage 2 book extraction remains an attributed historical record. This stage evaluates the reusable interpretations against independent professional and empirical material. **Source result** below reports the separate evidence at its actual reading scope. **Project disposition** applies the bootstrap’s aims, constraints and professional boundary; it is not a claim that the source author issued that recommendation. RETAIN, ADAPT, REJECT and RESEARCH here are analytic dispositions, not the PASS/FAIL vocabulary used for stage conformance.

A limited study can defeat an unconditional claim without showing the opposite is always true. An observed return or conversion increase can still be commercially unattractive after costs, and an effective tactic can still violate the project’s truthfulness boundary. Conceptual criticism is not experimental disproof. Professional guidance can justify a prudent production check without proving a universal effect size.

## 2. U01–U18: researched dispositions and unresolved claims

All eighteen inputs from Stage 2 are retained with their original IDs and questions. <a id="u01"></a>

### U01: O market selection and P score cut-offs

**Carried-forward question:** Which market/customer signals predict viable demand, and which scoring shortcuts conceal fatal conditions?

**Source result:** Professional market guidance separates demand, affordability, alternatives and reach. Interview and willingness-to-pay methods have distinct inferential limits. Sources: [S01](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s01), [S02](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s02), [S07](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s07).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN separate market/customer questions; REJECT an unvalidated universal score cut-off as sufficient approval to build or scale.

**Still unresolved:** Which signals predict viable demand in the particular business, and whether a proposed weighting predicts anything beyond its inputs.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Dated market inputs plus observed buying/workaround evidence from the intended population; validate a predictive score against outcomes before using it as a gate.

<a id="u02"></a>

### U02: O premium pricing and M low-price learning

**Carried-forward question:** How do price, proof, segment and maturity alter willingness to pay, adherence and learning transfer?

**Source result:** Hypothetical pricing measures can diverge from real payment; the net-distribution trial shows that payment need not increase subsequent use. Sources: [S07](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s07), [S08](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s08), [S32](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s32).

**Project disposition:** ADAPT premium, discount and trial choices to segment, proof and economics. REJECT the universal inference that a higher price necessarily improves commitment or outcomes.

**Still unresolved:** The demand, adherence and retention response at the actual proposed price and whether learning transfers from a cheaper early offer.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** A bounded, authorised comparison with transparent terms and downstream outcomes, not just initial acceptance; preserve scope and measure relevant costs.

<a id="u03"></a>

### U03: O Value Equation, bonus and naming effects

**Carried-forward question:** Which mechanisms have evidence, what are their limits, and can effects be separated from better underlying delivery?

**Source result:** Positioning and value frameworks structure decisions; bundling theory depends on valuation and marginal-cost conditions rather than a universal bonus effect. Sources: [S04](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s04), [S05](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s05), [S09](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s09).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN qualitative value, obstacle, packaging and comprehension analysis. RESEARCH causal effects of particular changes; REJECT treating the Value Equation as a calibrated score.

**Still unresolved:** The separate effects of naming, bundles, evidence and actual delivery improvements, including whether any effect persists after novelty.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Comprehension evidence followed, where justified, by a designed comparison that separates changes or explicitly estimates their combined effect with downstream economics.

<a id="u04"></a>

### U04: O/M guarantees, rebates and credit

**Carried-forward question:** How do remedies change appropriate conversion, refunds, misuse, contribution and long-term trust?

**Source result:** The return-policy meta-analysis finds different purchase/return responses by policy dimension. It does not test all performance guarantees or cash-versus-credit substitutions. Sources: [S11](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s11), [S22](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s22), [S44](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s44).

**Project disposition:** ADAPT remedies by obligation, deliverability, refund exposure and informed choice. REJECT treating a store credit as interchangeable with an existing cash entitlement.

**Still unresolved:** Local take-up, refund behaviour, misuse, cost and trust effects of the proposed remedy; legal permissibility and required wording remain fact- and jurisdiction-specific.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Explicit terms and qualified review, then observed remedy utilisation and retained contribution across an appropriate period; include worst-case fulfilment/refund exposure.

<a id="u05"></a>

### U05: L lead-magnet effectiveness

**Carried-forward question:** When does additional interest become incremental suitable customers after content/service and sales costs?

**Source result:** Ad-measurement evidence distinguishes caused purchases from attributed purchases; useful-content guidance supplies no guarantee that free information creates paid demand. Sources: [S12](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s12), [S13](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s13), [S14](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s14).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN a useful, bounded lead-magnet hypothesis; REJECT opt-ins or content engagement alone as proof of incremental, profitable customer acquisition.

**Still unresolved:** Whether the magnet improves suitable paid conversion enough to cover production, delivery, sales and maintenance costs compared with an appropriate alternative.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Compare direct-offer and magnet paths under a justified design, including downstream quality and all incremental effort; otherwise report causal uncertainty rather than invented lift.

<a id="u06"></a>

### U06: L quotas, testing cadence and open-ended effort

**Carried-forward question:** What sample/exposure and resource conditions make tests informative without fatigue, burnout or uncontrolled spending?

**Source result:** Statistical information requirements depend on the question and design; operational and incentive methods expose constraints that activity quotas can hide. Sources: [S24](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s24), [S34](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s34), [S35](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s35), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42).

**Project disposition:** ADAPT cadence and exposure to information value and resource limits. REJECT a universal daily quota, fixed number of attempts or unlimited-effort prescription as validation.

**Still unresolved:** The appropriate observation window, sample/exposure and sustainable effort for the specific channel, event rarity and team.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Estimate the precision or discrimination needed and available exposure, then predefine spend/time and stopping controls; record fatigue or quality costs alongside activity.

<a id="u07"></a>

### U07: L/M referrals, giveaways and affiliates

**Carried-forward question:** How should incrementality, adverse selection, disclosure and partner delivery/incentives be evaluated?

**Source result:** Referred customers can differ from non-referred customers, but an observed value difference does not identify the incremental effect of a reward programme. Sources: [S15](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s15), [S28](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s28), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42), [S44](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s44).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN partner/referral hypotheses; ADAPT incentives to fit, incrementality and delivery responsibilities. REJECT importing a reported referral-value uplift into a forecast.

**Still unresolved:** Reward-driven incremental demand, abuse, disclosure effects, participant selection and the partner’s ability to fulfil obligations.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** A bounded authorised programme with a baseline/comparison, duplicate/refund checks and partner evidence; legal review of promotions and endorsements precedes publication.

<a id="u08"></a>

### U08: M upsell/downsell sequences

**Carried-forward question:** What separates genuinely complementary value from cannibalisation, pressured buying and deferred dissatisfaction?

**Source result:** Bundling theory is conditional; activity-system and cost reasoning require a new paid component to be considered with its dependencies and consequences. Sources: [S09](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s09), [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39), [S45](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s45).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN complementary expansion and suitable lower-scope alternatives. REJECT forcing every customer through a predetermined monetisation sequence or manufacturing a missing necessity.

**Still unresolved:** Incremental customer benefit, cannibalisation, fulfilment cost and delayed dissatisfaction for the proposed sequence.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Trace the new component to a real need, compare incremental economics and observe subsequent use/returns; test alternatives without withholding already promised value.

<a id="u09"></a>

### U09: M continuity, fees and discounts

**Carried-forward question:** How do voluntary retention, recurring collection, switching barriers and customer value diverge across models?

**Source result:** Subscription research links some continued billing to inattention; provider metrics and customer-base models represent different aspects of renewal behaviour. Sources: [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S26](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s26), [S27](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s27), [S45](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s45).

**Project disposition:** ADAPT continuity analysis to voluntary choice, recurring value and cash obligations. REJECT billing persistence or a cancellation barrier as sufficient evidence of customer value.

**Still unresolved:** Which continued payments reflect active preference, forgotten subscriptions, payment recovery or contractual commitments in the actual cohort.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Reconcile value/use, active renewal/cancellation and billing events; test clear communication or value delivery, not deceptive friction, with appropriate customer safeguards.

<a id="u10"></a>

### U10: M/L thirty-day financing preference

**Carried-forward question:** What recovery horizons, working-capital demands and credit constraints apply to different business models?

**Source result:** Working-capital practice treats cash recovery as dependent on actual inventory, collection, payment and financing terms. Sources: [S22](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s22), [S23](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s23).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN cash-aware acquisition and recovery analysis. REJECT universally available interest-free credit and thirty days as a universally feasible or necessary payback horizon.

**Still unresolved:** The accessible financing, downside cash exposure and acceptable recovery horizon for the specific venture and owner.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** A dated cash forecast with verified terms, obligations and sensitivity; owner and qualified-finance review of borrowing or commitments rather than assumed approval.

<a id="u11"></a>

### U11: Conflicting LTV/CAC definitions across O/L/P/R

**Carried-forward question:** What professional definitions and calculation contracts prevent missing or double-counted costs and unsupported lifetime forecasts?

**Source result:** Customer-base research distinguishes settings and heterogeneity; Stripe’s published LTV is revenue-based. Management-accounting methods distinguish cost bases. Sources: [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S19](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s19), [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S21](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s21), [S27](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s27).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN a declared metric dictionary and reconciliation. REJECT comparisons that silently mix revenue, gross profit, contribution or different lifetime horizons.

**Still unresolved:** Which model, allocation and retention assumptions are defensible for the actual data; the magnitude and direction of estimation bias cannot be assumed.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Reconcile actual cohort costs/revenue, specify censored or unobserved periods, and compare suitable model assumptions. Use deterministic arithmetic but keep forecasts separate from observations.

<a id="u12"></a>

### U12: P/R measurement and test claims

**Carried-forward question:** What causal designs, sampling limits, stopping rules and uncertainty reporting are appropriate, including sparse data and long cycles?

**Source result:** Blocking, power and sequential methods answer distinct design questions; platform interference can affect identification. The metric-interpretation paper warns that metrics alone are insufficient. Sources: [S29](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s29), [S33](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s33), [S34](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s34), [S35](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s35), [S36](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s36).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN decision-led design and auditable interpretation. REJECT unplanned repeated fixed-horizon tests, treating non-significance as equivalence, or using one formula for every experiment.

**Still unresolved:** Appropriate estimand, sample unit, time window and inference under sparse outcomes, long cycles, attrition, interference or incomplete measurement.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Write the intended decision and design assumptions before exposure; use an appropriate method or specialist review, and classify a result as inconclusive when it cannot support the claim.

<a id="u13"></a>

### U13: R hypothesis-driven entrepreneurship

**Carried-forward question:** What independent comparative evidence supports the method, including null results, selection effects and boundary conditions?

**Source result:** Camuffo and coauthors study 759 firms across four trials, including the original trial plus three replications. Both groups received entrepreneurship training; scientific-method training altered termination and pivot patterns. The pooled revenue estimate was positive, but individual-trial estimates differed in precision. Sources: [S37](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s37), [S38](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s38), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN explicit hypotheses and decision-relevant tests. ADAPT the method to context; REJECT interpreting the findings as a guarantee of startup success or validation of every Lean tactic.

**Still unresolved:** Transfer beyond the studied training populations, durability, profit effects and the proposed mechanisms; conceptual critiques highlight risks of narrow feedback-led search.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Separate replications and alternative explanations, long-term economic outcomes and theory-driven tests that distinguish new value creation from optimisation of an existing concept.

<a id="u14"></a>

### U14: R prototypes and early adopters; P transparent presales

**Carried-forward question:** When does limited fidelity inform the real question, and when does it create false negatives or fail to generalise?

**Source result:** Professional discovery/design distinguishes understanding a problem from evaluating a solution; theory-led critique warns that visible short-run responses can miss a new opportunity. Sources: [S02](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s02), [S05](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s05), [S25](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s25), [S38](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s38).

**Project disposition:** ADAPT fidelity to the assumption. REJECT a mandatory low-quality public launch, pretending an unavailable product exists, or generalising early-adopter reactions without qualification.

**Still unresolved:** Whether limited fidelity exposes the intended value mechanism and how much results transfer to later buyers, channels and operating conditions.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Compare what the test omits with what the decision requires; recruit relevant contrasting participants and use honest prototypes/presales with appropriate safeguards.

<a id="u15"></a>

### U15: R growth engines and L acquisition expansion

**Carried-forward question:** How should growth mechanisms, saturation, retention and marginal channel economics be diagnosed together?

**Source result:** Customer heterogeneity, cohort behaviour, scarce capacity and cross-side participation supply different limits to growth; one successful acquisition average does not settle the next increment. Sources: [S13](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s13), [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S24](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s24), [S28](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s28).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN mechanism-specific growth diagnosis. REJECT unlimited linear extrapolation or treating the three growth-engine labels as a complete causal explanation.

**Still unresolved:** Marginal channel efficiency, saturation, ongoing value, cross-side availability and delivery/cash consequences at the proposed growth rate.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Trace the proposed incremental demand through qualification, retained value, fulfilment and cash; compare actual increments and re-evaluate when the limiting resource changes.

<a id="u16"></a>

### U16: P constraints and R Five Whys/small batches

**Carried-forward question:** What methods distinguish causal bottlenecks from symptoms and justify local versus systemic intervention?

**Source result:** Constraint and improvement methods require a defined process and observed checks; blocking can help distinguish changes from nuisance variation. Sources: [S24](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s24), [S33](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s33), [S43](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s43).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN diagnosis before intervention and the smallest sufficient responsible change. REJECT a Five Whys story as proof or assuming every problem has one local cause.

**Still unresolved:** The causal role of a suspected bottleneck and whether interactions or multiple constraints make a local intervention inadequate.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Observe flow and competing explanations, try a reversible discriminating change where safe, and measure quality/throughput effects. Escalate the scope only when justified.

<a id="u17"></a>

### U17: P incentives, performance load and sufficiency

**Carried-forward question:** Which organisational practices preserve quality and capacity, and how should owner objectives constrain growth advice?

**Source result:** Performance-pay evidence and multitask theory are complementary rather than a single verdict: incentives can raise measured output while measurement choices and task trade-offs matter. Sources: [S31](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s31), [S32](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s32), [S41](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s41), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42).

**Project disposition:** RETAIN explicit capacity, quality and owner objectives. REJECT both universal anti-incentive doctrine and growth-at-all-costs as defaults for every business.

**Still unresolved:** The local response to targets, workload and rewards, including unmeasured quality and the owner’s preferred trade-off between income, risk, time and scale.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Measure work and outcomes under declared objectives, review unintended consequences and approved obligations, and test a bounded change instead of assuming a compensation formula transfers.

<a id="u18"></a>

### U18: Branded vocabulary and ethical/legal surfaces throughout

**Carried-forward question:** Identify durable professional equivalents and researched safeguards; later legal design must use current jurisdiction-specific sources, not book statements.

**Source result:** Professional sources offer useful vocabulary, but provider definitions differ and regulatory sources address particular normative contexts. Sources: [S04](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s04), [S05](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s05), [S06](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s06), [S16](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s16), [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S27](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s27), [S44](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s44), [S45](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s45).

**Project disposition:** ADAPT to explicit domain-neutral meanings while preserving original terms in source lineage. REJECT rebranding that hides changed definitions or treats a book as legal authority.

**Still unresolved:** The applicable current legal requirements for a particular jurisdiction, audience, product and tactic, and any definition not resolved by available records.

**Evidence that could change the decision:** Use the terminology map and a qualified, fact-specific handoff in Stage 9; preserve the actual source meaning rather than silently changing it for easier implementation.

## 3. Criticisms, contrary results and transfer conditions

**Offer and acquisition optimisation.** The useful source-book insight is to make a meaningful exchange understandable and deliverable, not to assume all customers should pay more, all markets should be narrowly selected by a score, or more leads inevitably yield more profit. Pricing research, cost reasoning and causal acquisition studies supply independent reasons to distinguish perceived value, paid choice, attributed purchases and retained contribution. U01–U07 state those boundaries. Neither a counterexample nor an average effect supplies a new universal price, conversion or acquisition ratio.

**Monetisation and continuity.** Additional offers, discounts, remedies and recurring billing are candidate mechanisms. Their value depends on actual incremental benefit and obligations; customer inertia and service/cash commitments can make gross revenue a poor guide. U04 and U08–U11 therefore preserve the author’s actual tactic description while declining universal or misleading interpretations. The finding that an intervention can raise payment is not permission to conceal terms or impede exit.

**Broad mental models.** The Personal MBA’s own “mental models, not methods” framing, preserved in Stage 2, should not be converted into an authoritative answer to every business question. Professional cost definitions and causal methods add precision. A source’s convenient formula is not a substitute for an explicit cost basis, cohort or decision horizon. Incentive evidence also does not sustain a blanket position for or against rewards; U11–U12 and U16–U17 preserve conditional reasoning.

**Lean experimentation and strategic novelty.** [S38](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s38) argues that readily observable customer feedback can favour easy-to-test, incremental opportunities over distinctive theories. This is a conceptual criticism, not a finding that customer research is useless. Ries’s own qualifications about vision, quality and context remain in the Stage 2 corpus. The project synthesis is to state the opportunity theory, derive a test that could distinguish it from alternatives, and avoid confusing an easily measured proxy with the whole strategic question. [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39) adds stakeholder and activity-system coherence, rather than reducing business-model viability to customer enthusiasm.

U13 reports the independent training evidence. The full-paper review of [S37](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s37) included its intervention, sampling, individual/pool result differences and limitations; Table 6 on PDF page 19 was visually inspected rather than inferred from flattened text. A positive pooled result and imprecise individual estimates can coexist. No new trial, reanalysis of underlying data or causal mechanism test was performed here.

**Newer measurement research.** [S46](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s46), a 2026 working-paper abstract, is a counterpoint to treating every nonexperimental estimate as worthless. It describes prediction calibrated on experimental campaign evidence, not an arbitrary attribution rule. Its transfer and publication status limit its use here. The decision is to preserve methodological openness while requiring a declared estimation basis and validation context, not to select a new advertising tool.

## 4. Failure taxonomy

This is an independently expressed project diagnostic taxonomy informed by the cited sources and the 27 practice cards. It is not a measured distribution of business failures. Observable signals are prompts for investigation, not automatic diagnoses. A repair may be no action, local correction or a justified broader change; earlier validated decisions are preserved unless evidence implicates them.

| ID | Failure class | Signal and discriminating question | Owning layer and smallest responsible repair | Preservation and escalation | Independent basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F01 | Invented or untraceable evidence | Customer/market claims cannot be linked to a source, event or observation. Is the statement a hypothesis presented as fact? | Evidence layer: restore provenance, relabel assumptions, remove fabricated claims and request legitimate evidence. | Preserve valid observations; reopen decisions whose support depended on the fabrication. | S01, S02, S44 |
| F02 | Population or role mismatch | Strong feedback from people who do not buy, use or decide in the intended setting. Does the sample answer the actual question? | Discovery/segmentation: repair recruitment and role definitions, then reassess transfer. | Do not discard a working segment for an irrelevant sample; broaden the change if the assumed buyer is wrong. | S02, S07, S40 |
| F03 | Metric-definition or denominator failure | Ratios mix cohorts, event windows, currencies or cost bases. Is the intended quantity actually being measured? | Measurement/accounting interface: define populations and costs, reconcile source records and recompute. | Preserve business choices until corrected evidence warrants change; provider reporting quirks are not automatically business failures. | S16, S20, S27 |
| F04 | Attribution mistaken for causality | Ad-attributed sales rise without a credible counterfactual. Would those customers have purchased anyway? | Experiment/measurement: assess an appropriate comparison or explicitly bound unidentified effects. | Do not reprice a viable offer to repair attribution; change the channel only if evidence supports it. | S12, S13, S46 |
| F05 | Appeal without delivered value | The promise wins attention but intended users do not achieve the relevant outcome. Is the need, mechanism or delivery wrong? | Customer/value/delivery: inspect the failed link and test that specific explanation. | Preserve supported benefits; revisit the problem model when local delivery repair does not explain the failure. | S05, S25, S39 |
| F06 | Misleading or unfulfillable offer | Hidden obligations, unsupported proof, non-deliverable guarantees or obstructed exit. Does acceptance depend on misunderstanding? | Offer/claims with specialist handoff: stop the misleading commitment, correct facts and terms, obtain required review. | A conversion gain cannot preserve the harmful tactic; retain legitimate value and unaffected obligations. | S11, S44, S45 |
| F07 | Wrong price or economic basis | Conversion improves but contribution deteriorates, or a forecast relies on hypothetical payment. Which cost or demand assumption failed? | Pricing/economics: repair the identified price, scope or cost input and retest its consequences. | Preserve a proven customer and channel unless their mix is responsible; reject a universal premium-price solution. | S07, S08, S20, S21 |
| F08 | Profitable-looking growth with a cash gap | Obligations become due before usable collections. Is profit being confused with available funds? | Cash/terms/intake: correct timing, collection, reserves or commitment pace using a dated scenario. | Preserve validated demand and price where sound; financing decisions require owner/qualified review. | S22, S23 |
| F09 | Capacity and quality collapse | Backlog, rework or missed promises increase with sales. Where is the actual resource or quality constraint? | Operations/delivery: examine flow, adjust the limiting step or intake, and recheck the moved constraint. | Do not redesign every component to repair one bottleneck; escalate when linked constraints make a local fix inadequate. | S24, S32, S43 |
| F10 | Billing or expansion masks retention failure | Revenue holds while users leave or stop receiving value. Does continuation reflect active choice, expansion or inertia? | Value/renewal/measurement: separate cohorts and behaviours, then fix the implicated value or billing layer. | Preserve legitimate expansion; never make exit harder to manufacture a retention result. | S17, S18, S26, S27 |
| F11 | Ineffective or distorted intervention incentives | Activity/reward uptake rises but intended outcomes or quality do not. Is risk prediction being confused with response? | Intervention/incentive design: assess incrementality, task trade-offs and cost; revise or remove the misaligned reward. | Preserve beneficial customer relationships; significant compensation changes need the accountable human. | S17, S41, S42 |
| F12 | Partner or cross-side failure | One participant group grows but transactions, trust or fulfilment fail. Which responsibility or incentive is missing? | Partner/marketplace model: repair availability, matching, ownership or incentives, not just generate more traffic. | Preserve reliable partners and functioning sides; coordinated redesign is allowed when the dependency requires it. | S15, S28, S29 |
| F13 | Inconclusive or invalid experiment | Results are unstable, selectively stopped, sparsely observed or unable to distinguish alternatives. What inference is justified? | Experiment/measurement: correct design and interpretation, then gather adequate evidence or decide with uncertainty explicit. | Retain actual negative results and accepted evidence; do not invent a pass to keep the plan moving. | S33, S34, S35, S36 |
| F14 | Feedback-led search loses the opportunity theory | Many local tests improve a proxy but do not test the proposed new value system. What theory could the results falsify? | Opportunity/business model: state competing mechanisms and test a discriminating implication. | Preserve usable learning; allow strategic change when local optimisation cannot test or realise the opportunity. | S37, S38, S39 |
| F15 | Unnecessary broad redesign | A reporting, channel or handoff defect triggers replacement of validated customer, offer and delivery decisions. Which links are actually implicated? | Diagnosis/change scope: isolate cause, compare alternatives and choose the least extensive adequate intervention. | Keep unaffected accepted decisions; broader repair requires evidence of connected failure, not stylistic preference. | S24, S33, S43 |
| F16 | Unauthorised or misplaced professional authority | A recommendation is treated as permission to send, spend, bind terms or make legal/accounting conclusions. Who owns that decision? | Human/specialist handoff: stop the unauthorised action, state facts/questions and obtain the proper decision. | Preserve analysis without claiming execution or clearance; a missing integration is not permission to impersonate the specialist. | S31, S32, S44, S45 |

## 6. Decisions, rejected alternatives and exit assessment

**Retained:** connected business reasoning; customer/evidence discipline; explicit economic/cash meanings; source-grounded hypotheses; appropriate bounded experiments; multidimensional evaluation; constraint-led repair; human and professional ownership. The professional-practice map gives these responsibilities concrete inputs, evidence, artefacts, metrics, commitments, failures, repairs and model dependencies.

**Rejected:** a book-shaped architecture; treating source popularity as efficacy; applying an average research effect mechanically to a business; a mandatory premium price, thirty-day payback or survey fit threshold; counting passive billing as demonstrated value; treating all attribution as causality or all modelling as worthless; a universal business score; equating a positive test with legal acceptability; and replacing required research with a source list alone.

**Kept uncertain:** context-specific effect sizes and transfer, complete-playbook efficacy, customer/market facts not supplied, proposed business forecasts and jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions. Their precise questions and decision-changing evidence are recorded above. No empirical claim is invented to close them. No unresolved user-owned requirement or tool prerequisite blocks Stage 3’s research exit.

**Exit assessment:** every governing principle now has a defensible professional, empirical, methodological, theoretical or normative justification separate from the five initial books, with the type and limits stated. This is support for a disciplined business-building approach, not proof that every source heuristic is a law or that the future skills implementation works. Actual stage completion still requires the conformance checks and verified remote commit in [bootstrap progress](bootstrap-2-progress.md).

**Inputs for Stage 4:** the 27 practice cards; customer/buyer/user and alternatives distinctions; the terminology and evidence hierarchy; U01–U18 limits; failure classes F01–F16; and the bounded G01–G15 interpretations. Stage 4 must define the smallest useful customer/value/business-system representation, preserving observation versus hypothesis and avoiding a universal ontology. It must read its own original requirements before design. Detailed offer, acquisition, delivery, experiment and legal models remain with Stages 5–9; tools, skills, packs, examples, benchmarks, scaffold, installation and maturity remain with their separately assigned stages.
