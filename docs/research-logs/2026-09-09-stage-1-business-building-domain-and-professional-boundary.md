## 1. Purpose and Basis

This research log defines what `business-building-skills` means by **building a business**, what outcomes the project owns, which users it serves, which parts of the business lifecycle it covers, and where responsibility passes to adjacent capabilities, specialist tools or qualified humans.

The objective is to establish a domain boundary before extracting capabilities from the five-book source corpus or researching broader professional practice. The project must reason about a complete business system without becoming a universal business automation platform.

## 2. Domain Charter

`business-building-skills` provides reusable production intelligence for **forming, validating, operating, diagnosing and evolving a coherent business system**.

It helps an AI agent turn uncertain commercial assumptions into explicit business choices, evidence, experiments, operating models and corrective actions across:

```text
customer / problem
→ value proposition
→ offer
→ pricing / monetisation
→ acquisition
→ sales / conversion
→ delivery
→ retention / expansion / referral
→ unit economics / cash
→ experiment / evidence
→ diagnosis
→ smallest sufficient correction
```

The project owns the **business decision logic connecting these parts**. It does not own a universal integration runtime or the execution infrastructure of each specialist discipline.

> Business Building Skills owns business-system decisions, working business artefacts and learning loops. Specialist capabilities execute specialist work; accountable humans retain commercial authority and any professional judgement required for the decision.

### Standalone operation and family ownership

The project must remain independently installable and useful without Pactwright or another Production Skills repository. An agent may use ordinary authorised research, calculation and document tools to perform bounded business work. Installing a sibling skills repository must not become a prerequisite for basic customer-evidence analysis, offer design, economic modelling or experiment design.

Deep Research Skills, Legal Skills, UI/UX Design Skills, Software Engineering Skills and media-production capabilities are possible collaborators, not mandatory dependencies or claims that those integrations are already implemented. A qualified human or another suitable tool may fulfil the same handoff.

The central `production-skills` repository owns family contracts, registry/maturity, bootstrap rules and abstractions proven across independent domains. This repository owns its business workflow, artefacts, skills, commands, Extension Packs, examples, benchmarks and domain research. Shared abstractions require evidence of substantially the same need in at least two independent production domains.

Pactwright may consume business decisions and evidence to govern authorised delivery work. Business Building Skills does not recreate Pactwright's lifecycle governance.

## 3. What the Project Owns

The project owns reusable reasoning, workflows, working artefacts, diagnostics and evaluation concerned with business viability and coherence. These responsibilities do not imply one skill or file per item.

### 3.1 Opportunity framing

Own:

- expressing an opportunity as a testable business hypothesis;
- identifying the customer, buyer, user and beneficiary where they differ;
- describing the problem, desired progress or unmet demand;
- identifying the status quo and alternatives;
- recording important assumptions and uncertainties;
- deciding what must be learned before significant commitment.

Bounded evidence gathering and analysis with ordinary authorised tools are in scope. Commission deeper market investigation or specialist research when the decision needs it; do not duplicate a general-purpose research discipline.

### 3.2 Customer and problem model

Own:

- the declared target customer;
- the commercial problem or desired outcome;
- buying context, decision participants and relevant constraints;
- interview questions and evidence requirements for a business hypothesis;
- synthesis of supplied or legitimately obtained customer evidence;
- distinction between observed behaviour, reported preferences and assumptions;
- implications for offer, channel, sales path and delivery.

AI-generated personas, simulated interviews and synthetic respondents may help form hypotheses but must not be presented as observed customer evidence. Human research participation and customer contact require appropriate authorisation and safeguards.

### 3.3 Value proposition and offer

Own:

- defining the outcome the business promises to help create;
- connecting customer evidence to a credible value proposition;
- offer structure, packaging and scope;
- price presentation as a business-model choice;
- legitimate risk reduction and allocation;
- proof and trust requirements;
- identifying claims or terms that require substantiation or specialist review.

Deceptive persuasion, fabricated proof, false scarcity, dark patterns and unsupported claims are prohibited, not capabilities to delegate elsewhere.

### 3.4 Pricing and monetisation

Own:

- pricing hypotheses and structures;
- transaction and revenue mechanisms;
- subscription, one-off, usage, service, marketplace, licensing or hybrid revenue logic;
- expansion and repeat-purchase mechanisms;
- implications for margin, cash timing, retention and customer value;
- experiments that reduce pricing or monetisation uncertainty.

Do not own formal valuation, securities analysis, investment recommendations, statutory accounting or tax advice.

### 3.5 Acquisition and demand system

Own:

- acquisition strategy at the business-system level;
- candidate channel selection and channel-to-customer fit;
- lead-quality definitions;
- demand-generation hypotheses;
- acquisition economics;
- sequencing bounded demand tests before expensive scale;
- diagnosis across traffic quantity, traffic quality, message, offer and channel.

The project may brief and use authorised external execution tools. It does not build or administer advertising platforms, bulk outbound infrastructure, CRM systems or consent-management infrastructure as core responsibilities. Tool access alone does not authorise activation.

### 3.6 Sales and conversion path

Own:

- the commercial path from qualified interest to purchase;
- self-serve versus assisted sales choices;
- qualification and sales-stage assumptions;
- conversion bottleneck diagnosis;
- trust, proof and objection requirements;
- working sales-test briefs and scripts;
- commercial handoffs and measurement of progression.

Do not become a sales-engagement platform, CRM, dialler, email sequencer or autonomous negotiation system. A draft sales script is a business artefact; contacting prospects or making commitments is a separately authorised action.

### 3.7 Delivery and operating model

Own:

- how value is delivered at a business-model level;
- the relationship between promise, fulfilment cost and capacity;
- manual versus automated delivery choices;
- capacity constraints relevant to growth;
- fulfilment bottlenecks that undermine economics or customer value;
- operational implications of offer and pricing changes.

Do not own detailed software architecture, service implementation, supply-chain execution, workforce scheduling or other specialist operating disciplines. Business-level operating decisions are in scope; performing every operating task is not.

### 3.8 Retention, expansion and referral

Own:

- whether the business depends on repeat value delivery;
- retention and repeat-purchase hypotheses;
- expansion mechanisms;
- referral and advocacy loops;
- the relationship between continued customer value and acquisition economics;
- diagnosis of churn or weak repeat behaviour at the business-system level.

Do not treat retention tactics as a substitute for delivered value or assume repeat purchase is required for every business model.

### 3.9 Unit economics and cash model

Own enough managerial financial reasoning to evaluate commercial coherence, including, where relevant:

```text
revenue per customer / transaction
variable cost
contribution margin
customer acquisition cost
payback
refunds / returns
retention / churn
repeat purchase
customer lifetime economics
capacity cost
working-capital effects
payment timing
cash conversion
```

Required diagnostic coverage includes distinguishing revenue growth from contribution margin, acquisition payback from immediate cash availability, delivery capacity from demand, and modelled lifetime economics from observed customer behaviour.

The project does not own bookkeeping, statutory accounts, audit, tax calculations, payroll, treasury execution, formal corporate finance, personal financial planning or investment advice. Section 8 defines the financial-analysis boundary and minimum treatment of uncertainty.

### 3.10 Experiment and learning system

Own:

- assumption mapping and prioritisation;
- selection of the cheapest adequate test;
- measurable success, failure, stop and inconclusive conditions;
- execution briefs and bounded tool-assisted tests under explicit authority;
- evidence recording;
- separation of observation, metric, interpretation, decision and confidence;
- deciding whether to continue, revise, repeat, pause or stop;
- preservation of supported decisions.

The project should optimise learning relative to cost, risk and commitment rather than activity volume. An experiment is not successful merely because it was completed. Do not fabricate interviews, test participants, sales, conversion results or experiment outcomes.

### 3.11 Constraint diagnosis and business-model review

Own cross-system diagnosis. Given poor performance, identify the smallest plausible responsible layer before recommending broad change.

Illustrative investigation routes are:

```text
weak qualified demand
→ customer / channel / acquisition

qualified demand but weak conversion
→ offer / trust / sales path

strong conversion but poor margin
→ price / cost / fulfilment

healthy acquisition but weak retention
→ delivered value / onboarding / retention

healthy economics but cash stress
→ payment timing / working capital / growth pace

healthy demand and economics but service failure
→ delivery capacity / operations

inconclusive or contradictory evidence
→ measurement / experiment design / data quality
```

These are **diagnostic hypotheses, not causal lookup rules**. Check measurement quality, competing explanations and interactions before changing a layer. Record what is known, what remains uncertain and what evidence would distinguish alternatives.

Preserve validated decisions within the conditions supported by their evidence. Preservation must not freeze a decision whose assumptions have changed. Prefer the smallest sufficient correction, but permit broader redesign, pausing or stopping when evidence justifies it.

## 4. Intended Users and User Model

### 4.1 Primary users

The primary users are founders, solo business builders, small business owners, operators, early business teams, product/venture builders and consultants or agencies working on a client's business model.

The repository should remain usable by one capable operator rather than assuming a large specialist organisation.

### 4.2 Working contexts

| User/context | Starting inputs | Useful outcome | Accountable authority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder or solo builder exploring an opportunity | Problem or idea, available evidence, resources, constraints and desired business outcome | Explicit business hypothesis, uncertainties, cheapest adequate next test and decision conditions | Founder or business owner approves exposure, expenditure and commitments |
| Existing-business operator diagnosing performance | Current offer and operating model, relevant results, known constraints and accepted decisions | Evidence-based constraint diagnosis, bounded correction and preservation plan | Responsible operator within delegated authority; owner for material changes |
| Product or internal venture team testing a new proposition | Existing product/business context, target customers, commercial hypothesis and permitted budget | New-offer, segment or monetisation test that respects the existing business | Product/business sponsor and relevant specialist approvers |
| Consultant or agency acting for a client | Client brief, authorised evidence, scope and explicit delegation | Decision-ready alternatives, test briefs and specialist handoffs | Client or named delegate; consultancy access does not confer unlimited authority |

The human using the skills is not necessarily the buyer or user of the business being analysed. Distinguish those roles in the working business model.

### 4.3 Minimum engagement context

Establish the decision to be made, new versus existing-business context, target customer, evidence available, relevant jurisdiction, budget/cash/capacity constraints, accountable decision-maker and permitted actions.

Do not turn this into a compulsory long questionnaire. Use supplied information, label remaining assumptions and ask only for missing details that materially affect the decision or safe execution. Missing information may allow analysis while blocking a particular commitment.

### 4.4 AI-agent role

The AI agent is a **business-building copilot and production agent**, not the ultimate commercial authority. It may analyse evidence, generate alternatives, expose assumptions, calculate implications, produce working artefacts and design or execute bounded tests with authorised tools.

It must not silently create financial, legal, reputational or customer-facing obligations. Section 13 governs execution authority and approval.

## 5. Lifecycle Coverage

Business Building Skills covers both **greenfield business formation** and **existing-business improvement**. The following paths illustrate responsibilities, not a mandatory linear workflow for every business.

### 5.1 Greenfield path

```text
opportunity
→ explicit assumptions
→ customer / problem evidence
→ value proposition and offer hypothesis
→ pricing / monetisation hypothesis
→ bounded demand and sales-path tests
→ manual or low-cost delivery test where appropriate
→ economics evidence
→ retention / repeat evidence where relevant
→ repeatable business system
→ measured expansion where justified
```

Resist premature commitment to a complete product, brand system, acquisition stack or operating organisation while critical assumptions remain untested. Select the next adequate test by the current uncertainty and constraints, not a fixed sequence of tactics.

### 5.2 Existing-business path

```text
observed business problem
→ current-system model
→ evidence and measurement checks
→ constraint hypotheses
→ smallest responsible intervention
→ experiment / controlled change
→ measured result
→ preserve, revise, revert, pause or stop
```

Existing validated decisions are assets. A failed campaign does not automatically justify pricing redesign; a delivery-capacity problem does not automatically justify a new target customer.

### 5.3 Growth, stability and stopping

Growth work includes channel or segment expansion, increased acquisition spend, sales capacity, pricing and packaging changes, retention and expansion systems, fulfilment capacity, working capital, payback and cash constraints.

Growth is not the compulsory objective. The owner's goal may be sustainable income, improved resilience, better margins or a stable business within a capacity limit. Pausing an offer, rejecting expansion or stopping an unsupported proposition are legitimate outcomes.

Business-level continuation and stopping decisions are in scope. Formal insolvency, dissolution, restructuring, M&A, enterprise transformation, international tax structure and capital-markets execution remain specialist responsibilities.

## 6. B2C and B2B Applicability

The core model must support consumer and business markets without making either a universal template.

Both are represented through customer/buyer roles, value, commercial offer, acquisition, conversion, revenue, fulfilment, retention or repeat behaviour where relevant, economics, cash and learning.

Potential variations to investigate include:

| Context | Possible decision variables, not assumptions imposed by the label |
|---|---|
| Consumer proposition | Buying cycle, self-serve or assisted purchase, transaction volume, ecommerce conversion, refunds/returns, subscriptions and consumer-facing obligations |
| Business proposition | Buyer/user/approver differences, qualification, account-level economics, procurement, pilots, implementation effort, sales cycle, renewal and expansion |

Do not infer cycle length, sales motion or required metrics solely from a B2C/B2B label. Represent the observed buying and delivery model.

Extension Packs and pack authoring are part of the target family architecture. Stage 14 will determine justified specialisations and their boundaries; the core must remain useful without a pack. SaaS, ecommerce, professional services, marketplaces and subscriptions are candidate contexts, not a final pack catalogue or prescribed skill decomposition.

## 7. Strategy Versus Tactical Execution

> The project owns tactics needed to test, operate or repair a business hypothesis, but does not become the specialist execution system for every tactic.

| Business question | Business Building Skills responsibility | Specialist execution or handoff |
|---|---|---|
| Which customer segment should we test? | Segment hypothesis, bounded evidence analysis and decision criteria | Deeper research when required |
| Which acquisition channel should we test? | Channel fit, commercial hypothesis, economics, limits and test design | Advertising or outreach tools execute authorised work |
| What landing-page claim should be tested? | Claim hypothesis, draft proposition, proof requirements and success criteria | UI/UX, copy or creative production; specialist claim review where needed |
| Should we introduce a subscription? | Revenue mechanism, customer-value assumptions and economic implications | Legal, billing and software implementation |
| Why is conversion falling? | Measurement checks and cross-system diagnosis | Specialist repair of the identified layer |
| Should we change price? | Commercial hypothesis, downside analysis and experiment design | Approved implementation in billing or commerce systems |

Working interview guides, sales-test scripts, offer drafts and economic calculations are legitimate outputs. Producing one does not authorise publication, customer contact, expenditure or a live system change.

Use existing tools for execution where suitable. Do not create a CRM, ad platform, workflow engine or provider runtime to perform a bounded business task.

## 8. Financial-Analysis Depth

The project needs a **managerial and business-model financial layer**, not an accounting profession implementation.

It should reason about revenue models, price and volume, contribution margin, acquisition cost, payback, retention economics, refunds/returns, delivery and capacity costs, cash timing, working capital at a decision level, scenario comparisons, break-even conditions and commercial sensitivity.

It must not claim authority over statutory accounting, audit, GAAP/IFRS compliance, tax returns or optimisation, payroll accounting, investment suitability, securities recommendations, formal valuation, capital adequacy, regulated lending decisions or personal financial planning.

Analysing the business model of a financial product is distinct from providing personalised financial guidance to that product's customers.

### Financial evidence boundary

Distinguish:

```text
observed accounting data
management estimate
business-model assumption
scenario
forecast
calculated business metric
```

For decision-relevant calculations, record the source and date of inputs, currency and period, relevant customer/cohort/unit, cost inclusions, calculation basis and material uncertainties. Calculations should be reproducible with appropriate tools; do not silently compare incompatible units or periods.

Unknown inputs remain unknown or explicit assumptions. Use ranges and sensitivities where justified rather than false precision. Modelled lifetime value is not observed lifetime value; revenue, profit and available cash are separate quantities.

Do not introduce universal margin, payback or lifetime-value thresholds in Stage 1. Acceptability depends on the business, risk, capacity, available cash and stated objectives. Stages 3 and 7 will research and define the calculation and evidence contracts.

## 9. Marketing and Sales Boundary

Marketing and sales sit inside the business system but do not consume the project.

Business Building Skills owns the target-market hypothesis, commercial positioning, value proposition, offer logic, acquisition-channel choice, lead-quality model, sales-path design, conversion hypotheses, acquisition economics and business experiment decisions.

Specialist work includes finished brand identity, visual design, media and advertising production, specialist copy production, ad-platform configuration and bidding, CRM administration, email infrastructure, contact enrichment, outbound infrastructure and consent/suppression implementation.

Business Building Skills may provide working drafts, briefs, constraints and acceptance criteria, then evaluate returned evidence against the business hypothesis. Human sellers or authorised specialist systems conduct live sales activity. Those systems do not inherit authority to make unsupported claims or commitments outside the approved scope.

## 10. Operations Boundary

Operations are in scope where they determine whether the commercial promise can be delivered at the required quality, cost, capacity and cash timing.

The project owns business-level questions such as:

```text
Can this offer be fulfilled at the promised quality?
What is the delivery bottleneck?
What variable cost does the offer create?
Can capacity support the proposed acquisition rate?
Should delivery remain manual while uncertainty is high?
Does automation justify its cost and commitment?
Does growth create working-capital stress?
```

It does not replace manufacturing engineering, warehouse management, supply-chain optimisation, workforce-management systems, software architecture, site reliability engineering, clinical operations or regulated quality systems.

A future Extension Pack may specialise business decisions for an operating context. It must not silently absorb another discipline's implementation or professional authority.

### Minimum handoff content

A useful handoff states the decision or task, relevant evidence and uncertainties, accepted decisions to preserve, constraints and jurisdiction, execution/approval limits, expected deliverable and acceptance criteria. The returned work should identify results, limitations and remaining approvals so the business decision can be updated.

This is a responsibility boundary, not a fixed schema or new workflow engine. Stages 9 and 11 will refine specialist and execution contracts.

When a specialist is unavailable, prepare the brief and record the unresolved dependency. Continue unaffected analysis, but do not label blocked specialist work complete or activate a commitment that depends on it. Installing a skills package or generating a review document does not by itself supply professional sign-off.

## 12. Legal, Tax and Regulated-Activity Boundary

Treat legal and regulatory constraints as business-design inputs without presenting the project as the final legal authority.

The project may identify possible risk, record jurisdiction-sensitive assumptions, reject deceptive conduct, prepare facts and questions for review, preserve reviewed constraints and require the relevant approval before activation.

It must not fabricate legal certainty, present a jurisdiction-specific conclusion as universally valid, replace required professional judgement, recommend evasion or convert a commercial preference into a legal interpretation.

Potential review surfaces include:

```text
advertising and substantiation
consumer protection
pricing presentation
subscriptions and cancellation
refunds / guarantees
privacy and direct marketing
email / SMS consent
testimonials / endorsements
earnings or ROI claims
competition / antitrust
financial promotions
regulated financial activity
health claims
children / vulnerable consumers
employment / contractor classification
intellectual property
cross-border trade
tax
```

These are issue categories for later investigation, not jurisdiction-specific legal conclusions or an exhaustive compliance checklist. Stage 9 owns the detailed handoff design.

Legal Skills or other specialist tools may assist research, issue spotting and drafting. Where qualified professional judgement or formal authorisation is required, a generated artefact is not a substitute. Human commercial approval does not override truthfulness, safety or legal constraints.

## 13. Human Approval Points

Distinguish **analysis and preparation** from **external execution and commitments**.

| Activity | Authority boundary |
|---|---|
| Analyse authorised evidence, calculate scenarios, prepare alternatives or drafts | May proceed within the requested task; does not confer publication or execution authority |
| Run a low-risk, reversible operation | May proceed only within an explicit, applicable pre-authorisation |
| Create or materially change a financial, contractual, customer-facing, privacy or reputational commitment | Requires accountable human approval specific to that commitment, plus any relevant specialist approval |
| Proposed deceptive, unlawful or otherwise prohibited conduct | Must not proceed; neither user approval nor an automation policy removes the constraint |

Approval-sensitive commitments include publishing a new claim, introducing guarantees or refund obligations, material price changes, subscription/renewal terms, paid-media commitments, outbound activation, contracts, inventory/hiring/capital commitments, financing and sensitive-data collection or use.

A pre-authorisation identifies the accountable approver, allowed action and artefacts, applicable audience or system, spending and impact limits, validity period, safeguards and stop conditions. A tool credential, approved strategy or access to client data is not such an authorisation.

Reapproval is required when the proposed action exceeds those limits, changes approved claims or terms materially, introduces a new risk, loses a required approval or falls outside the authorisation's validity. Approval of one test does not automatically authorise scaling it.

Maintain a record of what was approved and what was executed. An explicit commercial approval cannot replace a required qualified review or authorise prohibited conduct. Later stages may refine mechanics without weakening these boundaries.

## 14. Quality Model

Business quality is multidimensional. Evaluate separate dimensions rather than one synthetic score.

| Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Customer evidence | Is the customer/problem model supported by attributable evidence? |
| Problem importance | Is the problem or desired progress commercially meaningful in this context? |
| Value clarity | Is the proposition understandable and relevant? |
| Offer strength | Are value, scope, proof and risk allocation credible? |
| Differentiation | Is there a reason to choose this proposition over alternatives or the status quo? |
| Trust / proof | Are claims supported by adequate evidence? |
| Pricing fit | Does pricing reconcile customer value, evidence of willingness to pay and business economics? |
| Monetisation coherence | Does the revenue mechanism fit buying behaviour and delivery? |
| Acquisition quality | Does acquisition reach suitable prospects within the relevant economics? |
| Conversion quality | Can qualified demand progress without misleading pressure? |
| Delivery quality | Can the promised value actually be delivered? |
| Retention / repeat value | Is continued value supported where the business model depends on it? |
| Unit economics | Are the relevant unit-level economics acceptable against explicit objectives and constraints? |
| Cash robustness | Can obligations be met under the modelled cash timing and downside conditions? |
| Capacity robustness | Can the operating model handle intended demand? |
| Experiment quality | Does the test address meaningful uncertainty with interpretable evidence? |
| Learning velocity | Does evidence produce timely, traceable decisions rather than activity alone? |
| Preservation | Are supported decisions protected, and reopened only for relevant reasons? |
| Legal / ethical robustness | Are choices truthful, with applicable constraints and required reviews respected? |

Attach evidence, uncertainty and applicability to assessments. Distinguish **not applicable**, **unknown**, **hypothesised** and **observed** rather than marking missing evidence as a pass. Not every dimension needs a numerical score.

Truthfulness, permission and required approvals are gates, not weaknesses that higher revenue can offset. Business-specific metric definitions, thresholds and benchmark rubrics remain work for later stages.

## 17. Boundary Tests

Use these tests when deciding whether a later capability belongs in the project.

### Test A — Does it change or evaluate the business system?

A capability that determines customer, value, offer, revenue, acquisition, conversion, delivery, retention, economics, cash, experimentation or business constraints is a candidate for ownership. Specialist implementation of an already-decided requirement is normally a handoff.

### Test B — Is the knowledge reusable across business types?

Core skills encode durable business-building intelligence. A model-specific method may belong in an Extension Pack. Do not freeze the core/pack decision before capability research.

### Test C — Does it require professional judgement or formal authorisation?

The project may frame the issue and support the decision, but cannot substitute its own output for required qualified judgement or authorisation. A specialist software system is not automatically the final authority.

### Test D — Can a specialist capability execute it better?

Provide a brief, constraints and acceptance criteria rather than duplicate a specialist discipline. This does not prevent ordinary bounded business analysis when no sibling skills package is installed.

### Test E — Is it a tactic without a business decision?

"Send emails", "run ads", "make a logo", "build a landing page" or "implement billing" alone is not sufficient justification for core ownership.

The project owns why, when, for whom, under what economics, with what evidence and how the result changes the business decision. Execution remains subject to section 13.

### Illustrative boundary checks

| Request | Expected boundary decision |
|---|---|
| Compare two offers using supplied sales and cost data | In-scope analysis; make assumptions and uncertainty explicit |
| Draft questions to test a customer problem | In-scope business experiment preparation; do not invent respondents or results |
| Build and deploy the complete billing system | Own commercial requirements; hand implementation to engineering |
| Activate a paid campaign because the test brief is complete | Do not infer approval; verify execution and spend authority |
| Issue a personalised investment recommendation | Outside the business-building mandate |
| Scale despite unsupported claims or unresolved required legal review | Do not activate; address the evidence or approval dependency |
| Continue a bounded business-model review without another skills repository | Supported; standalone use is required |

These checks clarify the charter. They are not claims of implemented or measured benchmark behaviour.

## 18. Decisions Made in Stage 1

1. **The domain is business-system production, not business-content generation.**
2. **Coverage includes new businesses, existing-business diagnosis, measured expansion, stability and justified stopping decisions.**
3. **Core applicability spans B2C and B2B without hard-coding a sales motion or growth objective.**
4. **The project owns cross-functional commercial decisions, working artefacts and bounded learning activity, not every specialist execution layer.**
5. **Standalone usefulness is required; other skills, tools and Pactwright are optional collaborators.**
6. **Managerial financial reasoning is in scope; statutory accounting, tax advice, investment advice and personal financial planning are not.**
7. **Marketing, sales and operations are in scope as business systems; their specialist platforms and implementation disciplines remain handoffs.**
8. **Experiment design, evidence handling, measurement checks and constraint diagnosis are first-class responsibilities.**
9. **Material commitments require specific accountable approval; bounded pre-authorisation does not waive legal, ethical or professional-review requirements.**
10. **Legal, tax and regulated concerns are surfaced and routed rather than treated as universal professional advice.**
11. **Quality remains multidimensional, with explicit uncertainty, applicability and non-negotiable gates.**
12. **Preserve supported decisions, investigate alternative causes and prefer the smallest sufficient correction; reopen or stop work when evidence warrants it.**
13. **Family contracts and shared abstractions remain centrally owned; domain design and future pack/benchmark responsibilities remain in this repository.**

## 19. What Remains Provisional

The following are deliberately unresolved:
