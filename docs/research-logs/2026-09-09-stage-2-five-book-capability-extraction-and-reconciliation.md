## 1. Outcome and evidence boundary

The available material has been organised into **24 source-concept records, 12 provisional capability families, an overlap matrix and 11 conflict decisions**. These are research categories, not a prescribed skill count or implementation architecture.

The synthesis preserves useful commercial methods while rejecting the assumption that the most aggressive offer, highest price, greatest lead volume or fastest collection of cash is automatically the best business decision.

**This is not a complete reading or exhaustive extraction of five books.** Offers has companion-checklist coverage; Leads and Money Models have description-level coverage; Personal MBA has eight authorised excerpts; Lean Startup has author methodology and articles. The source register distinguishes these access levels and records the remaining gaps. No empirical effectiveness claim is established by this stage.

The resulting design direction is:

> Choose the business decision first. Use source concepts as candidate methods, make their conditions explicit, test the relevant uncertainty and preserve evidence that supports the resulting decision. Do not organise the repository by book, author, chapter or branded framework.

## 2. Extraction and disposition rules

Source IDs such as O02 and P06 resolve to locatable material in the [source register](2026-09-09-stage-2-source-register.md). Concept IDs identify this project's paraphrases. Capability IDs join the extraction matrix to lifecycle, inputs, outputs, evaluation and risk responsibilities in section 5.

| Disposition | Meaning in this stage |
|---|---|
| RETAIN | Keep the bounded concept as a research candidate; not a claim of proven effectiveness |
| MERGE | Combine overlapping concepts into an existing capability rather than duplicate it |
| ADAPT | Keep the useful idea but constrain or change its application |
| REJECT | Do not adopt the specified universal rule or unsafe interpretation; preserve its source and rationale |
| RESEARCH | Source detail or efficacy evidence is insufficient for adoption; retain a question, not an invented method |

Source claims, our adaptations and proposed business measurements remain separate. Neither multiple books repeating a concept nor a numerical-looking framework establishes independent validation.

## 3. Five-source-family extraction

The following profiles describe the **inspected material associated with each book**, not unexamined chapters. Metrics, workflows and failure modes proposed by this project are detailed in section 5.

### 3.1 Offers: value, packaging and transaction risk

**Source position:** The companion material links market choice, perceived value, pricing and guarantees; the course description adds bundling and objection handling. Its pricing claims go beyond neutral offer analysis. [O01–O04](2026-09-09-stage-2-source-register.md#offers)

**Project adaptation:** Make an offer inspectable as customer outcome, included work, delivery conditions, evidence, price and buyer/seller obligations. Use perceived-value prompts to generate questions, not to calculate willingness to pay or manufacture proof.

**Assumptions and failure modes:** Claimed outcomes must be deliverable, the audience reachable and the price acceptable. Added components can increase fulfilment cost without adding valued benefit; a guarantee can create obligations the business cannot support. These are risks to test, not findings about a specific company.

**Abstract pattern:** Compare a narrowly scoped service with a broader package, preserving outcome evidence and estimating the extra delivery burden. This is an original illustration, not a book example.

**Overlap/tension:** Merge risk allocation with P05 and pricing with P04/M01. Reconcile stronger promises with evidence and bounded experimentation rather than treating persuasion as validation.

### 3.2 Leads: repeatable access to suitable prospects

**Source position:** The descriptions cover acquisition channels, lead magnets and other-party acquisition. They also explicitly distinguish profitable leads from volume; the book description assumes an offer already exists. [L01–L02](2026-09-09-stage-2-source-register.md#leads)

**Project adaptation:** Connect customer, offer, channel, qualification and sales capacity in a bounded acquisition test. A contact collected is not equivalent to a qualified prospect, buyer or retained customer.

**Assumptions and failure modes:** Audience access, permission, offer fit, costs and the ability to follow up require evidence. Optimising sign-ups alone may reward irrelevant interest. Delegation does not remove incentive, quality or approval constraints.

**Abstract pattern:** Test one permitted outreach route for a service, recording relevant responses and delivery capacity before expanding activity. No advertised script or playbook is reconstructed.

**Overlap/tension:** Qualification feeds sales and economics. Referral acquisition connects to continued customer value, but the two are not identical responsibilities. The volume/quality tension is partly resolved within the source itself.

### 3.3 Money Models: transaction sequencing and cash timing

**Source position:** The publisher synopsis describes deliberately sequenced offers and faster customer-funded growth. The course description promotes acquisition funding and recurring/expanded spending, but the detailed mechanics were not available. [M01–M02](2026-09-09-stage-2-source-register.md#money-models)

**Project adaptation:** Represent when value, payments, costs and obligations occur. Consider additional transactions only where they serve a relevant customer need and improve the supported economic model. A sequence is an option, not a mandatory ladder.

**Assumptions and failure modes:** Upfront collections are not automatically profit or cash available for unrestricted acquisition. Future fulfilment, refunds, weak repeat behaviour and capacity requirements can invalidate the intended funding logic.

**Abstract pattern:** Compare a one-off purchase with an optional continuing service using explicit delivery obligations and cash scenarios. Do not claim observed retention or apply an unseen book formula.

**Overlap/tension:** Merge cash reasoning with P06 and customer-level economics with R02. The source's short-payback aspiration is not a universal rule for every buying cycle or business objective.

### 3.4 Personal MBA: business-system coherence and constraints

**Source position:** The selected excerpts connect business functions, market screening, critical assumptions, price justification, risk allocation, cash timing, throughput and constraints. The constraint excerpt attributes its underlying theory to Goldratt. [P01–P08](2026-09-09-stage-2-source-register.md#personal-mba)

**Project adaptation:** Use this breadth to check missing responsibilities and cross-functional consequences. Preserve screening questions without turning their summed ratings into an automatic investment or launch decision.

**Assumptions and failure modes:** A plausible diagram, estimate or bottleneck story is not customer evidence. The chosen unit, objective and operating conditions must match the business. A local improvement may fail to improve the intended business outcome.

**Abstract pattern:** Examine a service whose booked demand exceeds delivery capacity and whose payments arrive after delivery costs. Compare bounded corrective options rather than simply seeking more sales.

**Overlap/tension:** This is not a competing general-business skill beside the other books. Its concepts merge into the same offer, economics, cash, learning and diagnosis responsibilities.

### 3.5 Lean Startup: uncertainty reduction and consequential learning

**Source position:** The author materials emphasise hypotheses, feedback, actionable measurement and course correction. The 2009 article distinguishes uncertain startup contexts from profitable small businesses; the 2015 article explicitly rejects equating learning experiments with careless or unsafe work. [R01–R03](2026-09-09-stage-2-source-register.md#lean-startup)

**Project adaptation:** Plan the learning decision, evidence and stopping conditions before choosing a test. Use the cheapest adequate representation, which need not be a reduced software product. Learning is consequential when it changes or supports a decision.

**Assumptions and failure modes:** Evidence must bear on the hypothesis. A small, selected sample may not support the proposed scope of a conclusion. Endless testing, moving success criteria or premature pivots do not become progress merely by using learning terminology.

**Abstract pattern:** Test demand for a new proposition honestly before committing to automation, then retain, revise or stop it according to the evidence.

**Overlap/tension:** Merge assumptions with P03 and diagnosis with P08. Learning does not excuse ignoring cash, customer obligations or a legitimate stable-service business model.

## 5. Provisional capability taxonomy and production implications

These are information and decision responsibilities, **not twelve proposed skills**. Skill decomposition belongs to Stage 13. The metrics below are proposed checks, not formulas claimed to have been extracted from uninspected book text.

| ID / capability | Lifecycle and required inputs | Working output | Proposed evidence / metric | Principal failure or misuse risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| K01 — Opportunity and customer framing | New proposition, segment change or review; customer observations, alternatives, access, resources and constraints | Customer/problem hypothesis and evidence gaps | Observed problem behaviour, accessible demand, buying conditions and willingness-to-pay evidence | AI personas or market scores presented as validation; buyer/user confusion |
| K02 — Value and proof | Proposition design or repair; customer goal, alternatives, delivery evidence and limitations | Value proposition with claim/proof requirements | Customer understanding, relevant outcomes and time/effort evidence | Perceived value mistaken for delivered benefit; fabricated certainty |
| K03 — Offer and risk allocation | Packaging, purchase and fulfilment; scope, cost, capacity, obligations and reviewed constraints | Offer brief, exclusions, remedies and approval dependencies | Acceptance alongside delivery burden, complaints, refunds and outcome evidence | More components without more value; unaffordable or misleading promises |
| K04 — Pricing and monetisation | New/change decisions; buyer context, alternatives, costs, transaction options and evidence | Pricing hypotheses and optional transaction structure | Observed buying response, contribution, mix and downside scenarios | Universal premium-price advice; unnecessary offer ladders; price changes outside authority |
| K05 — Demand and channel testing | Acquisition discovery or repair; audience, offer, permitted routes, cost/time limits | Bounded channel hypothesis and acquisition-test brief | Relevant reach, qualified responses, downstream progression and test cost | Impressions or free-resource downloads mistaken for commercial demand |
| K06 — Qualification and sales path | Interest through purchase; buyer roles, decision process, interaction evidence and capacity | Qualification criteria, sales-path model and working test materials | Stage conversion with denominators, cycle time, sales effort and reasons for loss | Optimising lead count; confusing self-serve with every sales motion; premature automation |
| K07 — Delivery and capacity | Fulfilment and expansion; promised outcome, work required, resources and performance | Delivery/capacity model and bottleneck hypotheses | Completion time, load, quality, rework and cost per relevant unit | Growing sales beyond deliverable capacity; local speed improvement that harms quality |
| K08 — Unit economics | Validation and operating review; cohort/transaction revenue, relevant costs and acquisition evidence | Reproducible economic model with known/assumed inputs | Contribution, acquisition cost, refund effects and sensitivity by compatible unit/period | Revenue treated as profit; omitted sales/support effort; speculative lifetime value |
| K09 — Cash and payback | Commitment and growth pacing; receipts, obligations, timings and uncertainty | Cash-timing scenarios, funding requirement and exposure limit | Cash balance path, collection timing, payback and downside headroom | Upfront cash treated as freely spendable; future obligations ignored |
| K10 — Retention, expansion and referral | After initial value and during expansion; customer behaviour, outcomes, costs and incentives | Continued-value, relevant expansion or referral hypothesis | Repeat/retention behaviour, incremental contribution, cancellation and referral quality | Forced recurring models; paying for apparent growth that does not add viable customers |
| K11 — Assumptions, experiments and learning | Across the lifecycle; decision, uncertainty, current evidence and authority | Assumption/test/evidence/decision record | What uncertainty changed, evidence quality, cost, time and stopping criteria | Synthetic evidence; post-hoc success criteria; endless testing without a decision |
| K12 — Diagnosis, preservation and correction | Existing-system review or structural change; accepted decisions, outcomes and competing explanations | Constraint diagnosis, preservation scope and smallest justified correction | Whether targeted evidence improves without breaking supported decisions | Symptom-to-cause lookup; unnecessary redesign; preserving invalidated assumptions |

### Common handling rules

Each decision-relevant measurement needs an attributable source, definition, unit or denominator, applicable period and explicit uncertainty. Unknown, assumed, observed and not applicable remain distinct. Financial calculations require reproducible arithmetic; exact contracts belong to Stage 7.

Every capability inherits Stage 1's truthfulness, authority, professional-handoff and standalone-use boundaries. Those controls do not become a thirteenth generic governance subsystem or require Pactwright.

### Selection policy when methods conflict

First preserve required constraints and approved authority. Then compare what the business is trying to achieve, what evidence supports each option, and the downside of being wrong. Prefer a reversible adequate test where the decision remains uncertain. Record an unresolved conflict rather than choosing the most confident author or aggressive growth claim.

No source framework can automatically override accepted customer evidence, known delivery limits, an explicit owner objective or a necessary professional review.

## 8. What is not adopted

Do not introduce book-shaped skills, proprietary chapter structures, mandatory offer ladders, a universal value equation, a universal business score, fixed growth targets or a compulsory software-product experiment.

Do not implement unseen scripts, playbooks or financial formulas from product-page promises. Do not generalise reported author results to expected user outcomes.

Source-specific concepts may still be useful for a bounded task once their details and applicability are established. Rejection of a universal rule is not a claim that every instance of the underlying tactic fails.

## 10. Candidate evaluation implications

These are original behaviour sketches for later example and benchmark design, **not implemented or executed tests**.

| Case | Input condition | Expected behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| E01 — Unsupported pricing confidence | A value-framework worksheet exists but buying evidence does not | Produce pricing hypotheses and a test; do not calculate a validated optimum from subjective scores |
| E02 — More leads, worse system | Contact volume grows while qualification or delivery deteriorates | Inspect quality, capacity and economics; do not recommend scale from volume alone |
| E03 — Cash received, obligations outstanding | Advance payments arrive before material future fulfilment and refund exposure | Separate receipts, margin and available cash; show unresolved inputs before a spending decision |
| E04 — Source access gap | An agent has only a course description but is asked for its detailed book method | Attribute the accessible description, preserve the gap and avoid invented steps or pages |
| E05 — Useful negative result | An honest test contradicts a commercially important assumption | Record a supported revise/stop decision rather than disguise the outcome as success or keep testing indefinitely |

| E07 — Stable bespoke service | The owner values sustainable income within a capacity limit | Evaluate that goal; do not force automated scale or recurring monetisation without evidence |

Each future case should trace to a capability, evidence requirement, failure mode and preserved decision. Selecting the final examples, commands, schemas and benchmark contracts remains the responsibility of their later bootstrap stages.
