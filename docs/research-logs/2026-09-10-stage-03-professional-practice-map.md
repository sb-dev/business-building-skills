## 1. Goal, inputs and method

Establish professional and empirical support beyond the five-book corpus for the business decisions within the [Stage 1 charter](2026-09-09-stage-01-domain-and-professional-boundary.md). The [Stage 2 taxonomy and validation queue](2026-09-09-stage-02-reconciliation-and-taxonomy.md) guide coverage but do not determine which outside findings are acceptable.

The original specification was reread from `main`, including its global principles, stage sequence and acceptance gates. Accepted Stage 1 and Stage 2 research and conformance records were reconstructed from `feat/bootstrap-2` at `901bc6469b247622c9b24d773cdcf98f8163d53c`. The five locally supplied Stage 2 archive files were checked against the live Git blob identities before use. No failed-attempt branch was consumed. The complete stage-specific acceptance checklist is retained in [conformance](2026-09-10-stage-03-conformance.md).

Research combined targeted searches for all named practices with searches for counter-evidence and the eighteen prior validation questions. Preference went to government/professional guidance, original framework authors, original studies and methodological papers. Searches for pricing and return effects, causal ad measurement, retention, working capital, platform interference, scientific entrepreneurship and incentive trade-offs extended beyond the initial book framing. The [source register and evidence hierarchy](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md) distinguish empirical studies, theory, practice, normative constraints and provider definitions, and state actual reading limits. This is a bounded, decision-oriented review, not a systematic review claiming exhaustive retrieval.

**Reading rule:** each card’s **Source finding** describes what the cited material supports. Its nine-field **Project application** is this repository’s independently expressed application of that material and the governing charter. The fields are proposed business responsibilities and checks, not workflows empirically tested in this bootstrap or prescriptions claimed verbatim from an author. No customer interviews, campaigns, payments or commercial experiments were run. Cheap evidence below names evidence a future authorised business task could obtain; it is not a claim that it was collected here.

### P01: customer discovery

**Related capabilities:** C01, C02, C17.  
**Source finding:** Interview guidance emphasises actual experience and neutral questions; I-Corps institutionalises discovery. Neither makes interview enthusiasm equivalent to purchase evidence. Sources: [S02](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s02), [S03](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s03), [S07](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s07).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose which customer/problem hypothesis deserves the next test, including reasons to reject it rather than selling the interviewer’s preferred answer. |
| inputs | Declared segment, buyer/user/payer roles, existing alternatives, research purpose, recruitment criteria, permitted data use and the decision currently at risk. |
| cheap evidence | Review existing support and sales records, then conduct a small consented round of interviews about recent behaviour. Seek contrasting and non-adopting participants. |
| working artefacts | Recruitment rationale, discussion guide, attributed observations, disconfirming cases and a hypothesis update; redact personal data not required for the business decision. |
| metrics | Coverage of relevant roles and contexts, recurrence and severity of observed problems, current workaround effort; keep frequency in a recruited sample separate from population prevalence. |
| commitment points | Approve external recruitment and data use before contact. Do not commit to a segment or production build solely because interviewees were polite or enthusiastic. |
| failure modes | Leading questions, founder-network selection, treating users as buyers, hypothetical willingness to pay, and searching only for evidence that supports the initial idea. |
| repair actions | Change the question or recruitment gap first; test a concrete choice next. Revisit the segment only when the evidence implicates it, preserving supported observations. |
| business-model dependencies | B2B discovery may require users, budget owners and procurement separately. Consumer payer and user can differ. Existing businesses can triangulate against actual churn and support. |

<a id="p02"></a>

### P02: market research

**Related capabilities:** C01, C02, C07.  
**Source finding:** SBA guidance combines secondary and direct research around demand, alternatives, pricing and reach. A market description is not a validated scoring model. Sources: [S01](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s01), [S07](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s07).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide whether a bounded market opportunity and reachable segment merit further commitment, not whether a large headline market makes a venture viable. |
| inputs | Geography, customer boundary, current alternatives, buying process, dated data definitions, capacity limits, market access restrictions and the financial decision being informed. |
| cheap evidence | Inspect official/public data and competitor offerings first; use targeted direct research only for material gaps that secondary evidence cannot answer. |
| working artefacts | Source register, market/alternative comparison, explicit estimation assumptions and a list of unresolved demand or access conditions rather than one attractiveness score. |
| metrics | Relevant customer counts or ranges, prices, reachable share and uncertainty; any market-size estimate exposes its population, period, exclusions and calculation basis. |
| commitment points | Do not purchase stock, hire or scale advertising on a total-market estimate alone. A market estimate does not authorise customer-data acquisition. |
| failure modes | Double-counted market segments, obsolete data, spending power inferred from need, ignoring the status quo, and replacing missing demand with an invented market-size figure. |
| repair actions | Repair the definition or source first; narrow the estimate and label uncertainty. Test reachable demand before changing a validated product or pricing structure. |
| business-model dependencies | Local services depend on catchment and capacity; enterprise sales on named accounts and approval cycles; marketplaces require separate supply and demand estimates. |

<a id="p03"></a>

### P03: positioning

**Related capabilities:** C01, C03, C09.  
**Source finding:** Dunford’s method starts with competitive alternatives and connects differentiated capabilities to value and a suitable market category. It is practitioner guidance, not a lift guarantee. Sources: [S04](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s04), [S01](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s01).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Select the context that makes the offer’s relevant difference intelligible to the intended buyer, including doing nothing or using a workaround as alternatives. |
| inputs | Observed alternatives, genuinely differentiated capabilities, evidence of customer benefit, best-fit segment, existing approved positioning and category expectations. |
| cheap evidence | Compare recent won/lost conversations and customer language; test comprehension of alternative positioning statements without simultaneously changing the offer or price. |
| working artefacts | Positioning rationale linking alternative, capability, benefit, segment and category; record rejected alternatives and proof needed for comparative claims. |
| metrics | Correct understanding, relevant consideration and qualified progression by segment; distinguish these from remembered slogans, raw clicks or the number of new category terms. |
| commitment points | Owner approval before changing market-facing positioning. New-category education requires an explicit effort and acquisition-cost hypothesis, not an assumption that comparison disappears. |
| failure modes | Confusing a tagline with positioning, inventing uniqueness, choosing an unfamiliar category without education capacity, or generalising from enthusiastic non-buyers. |
| repair actions | Clarify the confusing comparison or unsupported claim. Preserve proven customer/value choices unless the positioning test reveals that those choices themselves are wrong. |
| business-model dependencies | High-ticket B2B can require consensus across functions; familiar consumer categories may ease comprehension. Novel categories introduce education costs and uncertain sales cycles. |

<a id="p04"></a>

### P04: value proposition design

**Related capabilities:** C02, C03, C04.  
**Source finding:** The Value Proposition Canvas separates customer jobs/pains/gains from offered relief and benefits, and explicitly calls for evidence of fit rather than assuming it. Sources: [S05](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s05), [S07](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s07), [S25](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s25).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose which customer progress the offer should deliver and which components are necessary; distinguish perceived appeal from an achieved customer outcome. |
| inputs | Customer observations, alternatives, desired functional/emotional/social outcomes, constraints, evidence of outcomes, and costs or burdens imposed on the customer. |
| cheap evidence | Use concept comprehension or a bounded service demonstration to test the uncertain link. Check whether the chosen fidelity actually exposes the benefit being evaluated. |
| working artefacts | Value hypothesis, component-to-need traceability, expected customer effort and time, evidence gaps and clearly bounded outcome claims. |
| metrics | Observed task/outcome success, customer effort and time-to-value, plus willingness to choose or pay when appropriate; do not calculate a fictitious universal value score. |
| commitment points | Approve outcome promises only after matching the strength of evidence and delivery ability. A mapped gain is still a hypothesis until tested. |
| failure modes | Invented customer jobs, unsupported promises, confusing satisfaction with willingness to pay, or adding impressive components that do not improve the relevant outcome. |
| repair actions | Remove or retest the unsupported benefit/component first. Reassess the problem model when repeated evidence rejects the proposed progress, rather than merely polishing claims. |
| business-model dependencies | Service outcomes can require customer participation; software value may depend on integration; financial or health-related benefits require stronger substantiation and specialist boundaries. |

### P05: pricing

**Related capabilities:** C05, C15, C16.  
**Source finding:** Willingness-to-pay studies show hypothetical bias and heterogeneity. The net experiment is a counterexample to a universal claim that charging necessarily improves use. Sources: [S07](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s07), [S08](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s08), [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S32](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s32).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Compare a price, unit, package or payment structure against demand, delivered value, contribution and owner objectives without assuming the highest price is best. |
| inputs | Actual or hypothesised willingness to pay, alternatives, cost basis, delivery capacity, payer, renewal/payment terms, approved prices and exposure to refunds. |
| cheap evidence | Use stated-price research for exploration, then a small honest purchase/quote test where authorised. Hold other material variables stable or design their joint comparison deliberately. |
| working artefacts | Pricing options, testable demand assumptions, cost floor/constraint analysis, total-price disclosure and a decision record separating price level from timing and scope. |
| metrics | Qualified acceptance, contribution per opportunity/customer, refund and collection outcomes, and downstream usage/retention; label survey responses as hypothetical. |
| commitment points | Approve published prices, discounts and contractual changes. A low-price discovery result does not establish that the same audience will buy at a premium. |
| failure modes | Copying survey willingness to pay into forecasts, equating premium price with adherence, hidden renewal prices, uncosted service labour, and confusing faster cash with larger profit. |
| repair actions | Correct the price/scope/payment assumption responsible for the failure; retest transfer between segments or price levels. Do not apply a pooled research percentage mechanically. |
| business-model dependencies | Usage, subscription, one-time and service pricing expose different uncertainty. Account-level B2B quotes may be negotiated; consumer tests need transparent and appropriate treatment. |

<a id="p06"></a>

### P06: revenue models

**Related capabilities:** C04, C06, C13, C15.  
**Source finding:** Business-model frameworks cover interdependent activities; platform and bundling theories explain particular conditions, not a universal requirement to use four offer types. Sources: [S06](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s06), [S09](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s09), [S28](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s28), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Select how a defined payer exchanges value and how additional revenue lines fit delivery, incentives and economics; retain a single mechanism when sufficient. |
| inputs | Customer and payer roles, value delivered, pricing unit, transaction sequence, costs, cash timing, counterparties and existing accepted revenue commitments. |
| cheap evidence | Compare one mechanism’s economics and a limited real quote/purchase decision before layering upsells, advertising, licensing or a marketplace around an unproven offer. |
| working artefacts | Revenue-mechanism comparison, obligation/cost map, sequencing rationale, dependency assumptions and criteria for accepting or declining an additional revenue line. |
| metrics | Incremental contribution and collections, cannibalisation, attach rates, fulfilment costs and continued value; count revenue for the correct party rather than gross ecosystem volume. |
| commitment points | Approve new commercial obligations and incentives. Adding a paid step must not remove previously promised value or consume an existing refund entitlement. |
| failure modes | More offers mistaken for more profit, manufactured customer dependencies, hidden mandatory add-ons, ignoring partners’ incentives and importing zero-marginal-cost bundling logic into costly services. |
| repair actions | Remove or revise the failing step while preserving a working core exchange. Reassess the wider model only when dependencies make a local repair insufficient. |
| business-model dependencies | Marketplaces distinguish gross merchandise value from take revenue; subscriptions require recurring value; professional services are capacity-bound; information bundles differ from stocked physical goods. |

### P07: B2B sales

**Related capabilities:** C08, C09, C10, C22.  
**Source finding:** SPIN supplies a consultative question structure; CRM documentation makes stage definitions explicit. Neither tool usage nor a seller’s stage label proves buyer commitment. Sources: [S10](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s10), [S16](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s16), [S31](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s31).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose an appropriate path from a qualified problem to an informed business purchase, identifying the people and evidence needed for agreement. |
| inputs | Problem evidence, role map, budget/process constraints, alternatives, qualification criteria, scope, proof, procurement/security dependencies and seller delivery capacity. |
| cheap evidence | Review actual lost/stalled opportunities and observe a few authorised discovery conversations. Test the next commitment, such as a scoped paid pilot, rather than automating outreach first. |
| working artefacts | Buying-process and stakeholder map, qualification rationale, proposal assumptions, mutual next step and stage-exit evidence; distinguish seller activity from buyer action. |
| metrics | Qualified opportunity progression, time in stage, win/loss reasons, sales effort, deal contribution and collection delay; use cohort definitions for long sales cycles. |
| commitment points | Human approval before sending proposals, offering concessions or binding scope. Specialist review is required for material legal, technical or security claims. |
| failure modes | Counting meetings as qualified pipeline, single-contact dependency, aggressive problem amplification, unsupported ROI promises, and closing work the delivery team cannot fulfil. |
| repair actions | Repair the missing decision-maker, proof or scope boundary. Requalify rather than intensifying pressure when no suitable purchase exists; preserve evidence-backed price and segment choices. |
| business-model dependencies | Enterprise, owner-led small business and channel-led sales have different authority and procurement paths. High-ticket services also expose utilisation and long receivables. |

### P08: consumer conversion

**Related capabilities:** C04, C09, C10, C12.  
**Source finding:** Return-policy research distinguishes several dimensions; subscription research separates passive billing from active choice. Conversion alone cannot establish customer welfare or viable economics. Sources: [S11](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s11), [S26](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s26), [S45](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s45).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose the smallest improvement to an informed purchase or renewal path, considering suitability, comprehension, returns and customer value rather than only checkout completion. |
| inputs | Funnel events with matched populations, customer objections, total price and terms, delivery constraints, complaints, return reasons and allowed experimentation scope. |
| cheap evidence | Observe a bounded set of checkout tasks or inspect abandonment/support evidence; use a controlled change when volume and permissions support an interpretable comparison. |
| working artefacts | Funnel diagnosis, comprehension findings, claims/terms inventory, alternative explanations and an experiment decision record with guardrails beyond conversion. |
| metrics | Eligible-user conversion, net retained purchases, refund/chargeback exposure, support burden and later value; separate initial acceptance from informed continued use. |
| commitment points | Approve checkout, guarantee, renewal and cancellation changes before release. A statistical increase cannot authorise a hidden term or obstructed exit. |
| failure modes | Dark patterns, hidden charges, a misleading trial, changed traffic mix, multiple simultaneous untracked changes and counting eventual refunds as durable sales. |
| repair actions | Correct the misleading or failing step; remeasure downstream outcomes. Keep validated positioning and product decisions unless evidence identifies them as the cause. |
| business-model dependencies | One-time ecommerce emphasises delivery/returns; subscriptions include recurring consent and renewal; products involving vulnerable users require additional review and safeguards. |

### P09: demand generation

**Related capabilities:** C07, C08, C14, C15.  
**Source finding:** Market evidence, paid-search experiments and referral research all show why reach, customer mix and incremental commercial effects must be distinguished. Sources: [S01](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s01), [S12](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s12), [S13](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s13), [S15](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s15).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Select a demand-generation hypothesis appropriate to the audience and current business constraint, not a mandate to operate every possible channel. |
| inputs | Reachable customers, relevant need, existing awareness, channel access and consent constraints, accepted offer, selling effort, economics and capacity. |
| cheap evidence | Inspect existing acquisition and lost-customer records; run one bounded channel/message test after permissions, rather than building a multichannel automation system. |
| working artefacts | Channel comparison with rationale, target population, path to qualification, attribution limitations, resource cap and stop or expansion decision criteria. |
| metrics | Reach, qualified response, incremental suitable customers where identifiable, full acquisition/sales effort and later customer contribution; report uncertainty separately from platform attribution. |
| commitment points | Owner approval for contact, spend or scaled exposure. Verify sales and delivery can absorb the proposed demand; avoid treating campaign access as permission. |
| failure modes | Volume without fit, channel popularity substituted for audience evidence, repeated contact without permission, and acquisition into negative economics or unavailable capacity. |
| repair actions | Repair targeting, message or channel at the implicated layer. Preserve a validated offer when the channel is wrong; revisit the customer hypothesis only with supporting evidence. |
| business-model dependencies | Outbound, paid, organic, community and partner channels carry distinct effort, latency and control. Human services cannot automatically convert unlimited demand into deliverable revenue. |

### P10: performance marketing

**Related capabilities:** C07, C15, C19.  
**Source finding:** Experimental comparisons expose attribution bias; paid-search effects vary across customers. New experimentally trained prediction is promising but remains domain- and model-dependent. Sources: [S12](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s12), [S13](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s13), [S34](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s34), [S46](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s46).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide whether to maintain, stop or expand paid acquisition based on incremental economics and feasible uncertainty, rather than a reported return ratio alone. |
| inputs | Spend and exposure data, audience/cohort definitions, conversion and fulfilment outcomes, cost basis, attribution settings, test design, sales lag and available cash. |
| cheap evidence | Audit tracking and conversions first; use a proportionate holdout or other justified design where feasible. When data are sparse, bound exposure and keep causality unresolved. |
| working artefacts | Campaign hypothesis, measurement/attribution contract, incremental-effect estimate or explicit identification limit, contribution/cash scenario and a documented spending decision. |
| metrics | Incremental customers or contribution per cost, uncertainty, marginal versus average acquisition cost, refunds and retention; distinguish sales attributed to ads from sales caused by ads. |
| commitment points | Approve spend and targeting. Scaling requires cash/capacity checks; an underpowered result does not establish zero effect or justify unbounded further spending. |
| failure modes | Retargeting loyal customers and claiming every purchase, ignoring sales cost, mixing revenue and profit in ROAS, extrapolating past average efficiency, and uncontrolled optional stopping. |
| repair actions | Repair tracking, estimation or the failing audience/campaign. Use experimental calibration only when its assumptions and transfer are credible; otherwise report a decision with explicit uncertainty. |
| business-model dependencies | Long-cycle B2B needs delayed outcomes; ecommerce includes returns; subscriptions need retained contribution; privacy or platform restrictions may limit measurement and should remain visible. |

### P11: content / organic acquisition

**Related capabilities:** C07, C08, C09.  
**Source finding:** Google’s people-first guidance is platform-specific publishing advice. It does not establish that rankings, traffic or a content calendar will produce incremental suitable buyers. Sources: [S14](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s14), [S04](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s04), [S12](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s12).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide which useful audience problem merits content and how that content supports an appropriate next commercial step without becoming search-volume production for its own sake. |
| inputs | Observed audience questions, authentic expertise or evidence, competitive alternatives, search/distribution context, creative capacity, proof constraints and a relevant offer. |
| cheap evidence | Inspect existing search/support questions and distribution performance, then publish a bounded authorised asset or series with a clear learning objective and realistic observation window. |
| working artefacts | Content hypothesis and brief, source/proof requirements, intended audience and next step, distribution rationale, production-effort record and interpretation limitations. |
| metrics | Relevant consumption and qualified progression, assistance to actual buyer decisions, production/maintenance effort and contribution where identifiable; avoid equating impressions with demand. |
| commitment points | Approve publication and claims; obtain appropriate creative/research review. A forecast must account for uncertain time to reach an audience rather than assuming free traffic. |
| failure modes | Search-first filler, fabricated expertise, unsupported case studies, mass publishing without a need, follower counts treated as buyer evidence and ignoring opportunity cost. |
| repair actions | Improve the failing information, distribution or audience fit rather than multiplying output. Preserve useful assets and a working offer when the distribution hypothesis fails. |
| business-model dependencies | Expert B2B content may assist long sales cycles; consumer content may need scale; open-source education can serve users who never become buyers without being valueless. |

### P12: referrals / partnerships

**Related capabilities:** C08, C14, C22.  
**Source finding:** Referral-customer value associations are not the same as an incremental programme effect. Partner incentives and cross-side dependencies need explicit examination. Sources: [S15](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s15), [S28](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s28), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide whether and how a referral, affiliate or delivery partnership creates additional suitable demand or value after rewards, coordination and counterparty risk. |
| inputs | Existing customer outcomes, referral baseline, partner audience and incentives, reward terms, delivery responsibilities, disclosure requirements, capacity and counterfactual acquisition paths. |
| cheap evidence | Review organic referrals and a few partner/customer journeys; compare a bounded authorised programme against a credible baseline or holdout before expanding rewards. |
| working artefacts | Partner-fit rationale, responsibility and incentive map, reward/attribution rules, duplicate/refund handling, evidence requirements and escalation/exit conditions. |
| metrics | Incremental qualified acquisitions where estimable, reward and service costs, retention/contribution, fraud/duplicate rates and partner delivery outcomes; separate correlation from programme effect. |
| commitment points | Approve commercial agreements, rewards, data sharing and public endorsements; legal questions about promotions or disclosures are routed rather than assumed resolved. |
| failure modes | Rewarding existing demand, fraudulent self-referrals, misaligned incentives, low-fit giveaway entrants, uncosted partner fulfilment and dependence on an unreliable counterparty. |
| repair actions | Correct the reward, eligibility or partner boundary; stop the unsafe mechanism while preserving legitimate customer referrals. Replace a partner only when the evidence implicates that relationship. |
| business-model dependencies | B2B partners can own implementation or procurement access; consumer referral programmes need abuse controls; platforms must examine effects on both participant groups. |

### P13: sales operations

**Related capabilities:** C08, C10, C19, C22.  
**Source finding:** CRM lifecycle documentation distinguishes operational states and configurable transitions. Incentive theory cautions that a measurable activity target can displace other necessary work. Sources: [S16](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s16), [S10](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s10), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Define how sales evidence, ownership and handoffs support reliable decisions without turning the skills repository into a CRM or automatic prospecting engine. |
| inputs | Current pipeline definitions, record provenance, stage timestamps, qualification rules, owner authority, contact permissions, sales capacity and compensation incentives. |
| cheap evidence | Audit a bounded sample of stalled, won and lost records against actual buyer events; reconcile duplicate or stale states before forecasting from them. |
| working artefacts | Stage-definition and ownership map, required buyer evidence, data-quality findings, handoff criteria and a bounded correction brief for the authorised CRM operator. |
| metrics | Stage progression, age, conversion with cohort/period definitions, response effort, forecast error and data completeness; activity counts remain separate from customer commitments. |
| commitment points | Approve changes to records, qualification policy and incentives. The business analysis may propose a correction but does not mutate a live system by itself. |
| failure modes | Stages advanced without buyer evidence, denominator drift, inflated pipeline, poor handoffs, missing loss reasons and incentives that encourage unsuitable deals. |
| repair actions | Fix definitions, records or a handoff first, then reassess the forecast. Avoid replacing the whole sales model because of a reporting or hygiene defect. |
| business-model dependencies | Self-serve businesses may need minimal pipeline machinery; enterprise selling needs account/role context and long lags; solo services must include sales time in capacity. |

<a id="p14"></a>

### P14: customer success / retention

**Related capabilities:** C11, C12, C13, C19.  
**Source finding:** Retention intervention response differs from churn risk, and continued payment can reflect inattention. Contractual and repeat-purchase businesses also observe departure differently. Sources: [S17](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s17), [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S19](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s19), [S26](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s26).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose whether an onboarding, service or retention change can improve delivered value and appropriate continued use; avoid indiscriminate discounts to every predicted churner. |
| inputs | Cohorts, usage/outcome evidence, support and cancellation reasons, contract/renewal dates, intervention history, service costs and the customer’s actual objectives. |
| cheap evidence | Inspect a small set of early failures and successful journeys; test a targeted value-delivery intervention with a suitable comparison before broad incentives. |
| working artefacts | Onboarding/value-path diagnosis, cohort retention analysis, intervention hypothesis, expected incremental costs and a record of voluntary versus billing-only continuation. |
| metrics | Time-to-value, achieved outcomes, retention/repeat behaviour, incremental intervention response, support effort and contribution; report logo, revenue and activity retention separately. |
| commitment points | Approve service commitments and retention offers. Respect a customer’s choice to leave; unresolved data-use or cancellation questions block the affected action. |
| failure modes | Churn-risk scores mistaken for treatment effects, blanket discounting, aggregate growth masking cohort decay, coerced continuation and paid-but-unused accounts treated as proof of value. |
| repair actions | Fix the implicated onboarding or delivery gap; test whether a retention offer changes behaviour economically. Preserve a working acquisition channel unless its customer mix is responsible. |
| business-model dependencies | Subscriptions have observable renewal/cancellation events; noncontractual purchases require models of repeat timing; B2B accounts can expand in revenue while losing users or value. |

### P15: unit economics

**Related capabilities:** C15, C16, C19.  
**Source finding:** Accounting guidance distinguishes relevant costs and contribution; customer-base models and billing documentation show that a shared LTV label can conceal different quantities. Sources: [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S19](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s19), [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S21](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s21), [S27](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s27).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide whether a transaction, customer cohort or service engagement supports the proposed business action after a declared cost basis and uncertainty are applied. |
| inputs | Unit and period, gross/net revenue, discounts/refunds, variable delivery/support/sales costs, acquisition allocation, retention evidence, capacity and any lifetime-model assumptions. |
| cheap evidence | Reconcile a small set of actual invoices, fulfilment effort and acquisition records; calculate a transparent cohort scenario before trusting a summary dashboard or lifetime multiple. |
| working artefacts | Metric dictionary, cost-inclusion reconciliation, reproducible calculation and sensitivity analysis; explicitly separate realised cohort contribution from estimated future lifetime value. |
| metrics | Gross and contribution margins on stated bases, acquisition cost, realised payback and retention; compare ratios only when their units, populations and cost treatment align. |
| commitment points | No scale decision based on an undefined LTV/CAC ratio. Qualified accounting input owns statutory treatment; deterministic calculation supports, but cannot validate, uncertain assumptions. |
| failure modes | Double-counted costs, omitted founder time, revenue LTV compared with margin LTV, unstable churn extrapolation, misallocated acquisition cost and confusing marginal with total-business viability. |
| repair actions | Repair definitions or input data before changing the business. Show a range or unknown when lifetime evidence is insufficient; preserve a profitable core while isolating a failing cohort. |
| business-model dependencies | SaaS infrastructure/AI costs can vary with use; services consume human capacity; ecommerce needs returns/shipping; marketplaces distinguish participant revenue from platform revenue. |

### P16: management accounting

**Related capabilities:** C11, C15, C16, C21.  
**Source finding:** Relevant-cost, cost-volume-profit and throughput methods answer different managerial questions. Their assumptions must be explicit rather than merged into one universal profitability formula. Sources: [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S21](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s21), [S24](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s24).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose among capacity, product-mix, make/buy or continuation options using decision-relevant future consequences while checking whole-business sufficiency separately. |
| inputs | Decision horizon, alternative uses of resources, committed versus avoidable costs, demand assumptions, price/sales mix, bottleneck time and owner objectives. |
| cheap evidence | Inspect a bounded cost/capacity worksheet and actual operating records; compare the alternatives with a deterministic scenario before redesigning the accounting system. |
| working artefacts | Decision-cost bridge, contribution/break-even scenarios, limiting-resource comparison, assumptions and sensitivity record; keep these distinct from financial statements. |
| metrics | Incremental contribution/cash, break-even under stated assumptions, capacity consumption and headroom; record how fixed/variable classifications change by horizon. |
| commitment points | Owner approval before staffing, procurement or service-mix commitments; an accountant determines formal treatment. Do not use short-run contribution to excuse persistent full-cost losses. |
| failure modes | Sunk costs driving future choices, allocated overhead treated as avoidable cash, ignored opportunity cost, linear models outside their relevant range and unstable sales mix. |
| repair actions | Restate the decision and relevant horizon, repair cost classification, then recompute. Change operating choices only when the corrected comparison supports them. |
| business-model dependencies | Inventory manufacturing, digital delivery and consulting use different resources and cost structures; throughput accounting’s material-cost emphasis is not a universal service-cost rule. |

### P17: cash flow / working capital

**Related capabilities:** C15, C16, C21.  
**Source finding:** Working-capital guidance and cash forecasting treat liquidity and profitability as separate concerns, with model-specific timing between inventory, collections and payments. Sources: [S22](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s22), [S23](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s23).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide whether a growth pace, payment structure or fulfilment commitment is fundable through its cash cycle, not merely profitable on a spreadsheet. |
| inputs | Opening usable cash, contractual receipt/payment dates, overdue receivables, inventory lead times, payroll and approved tax inputs, refund exposure and verified financing terms. |
| cheap evidence | Reconcile bank movements and a small set of obligations; construct a dated rolling forecast covering the relevant cycle and a credible downside before adding commitments. |
| working artefacts | Cash calendar, working-capital assumptions, base/downside balances, funding gap and a bounded recommendation; separate prepayment proceeds from remaining delivery obligations. |
| metrics | Lowest projected cash balance, collection delay, inventory/receivable/payable days where applicable, realised cash payback and refund/fulfilment reserve sensitivity. |
| commitment points | Owner approval for credit, changed payment terms, inventory or hiring. Never assume every business has interest-free thirty-day credit or that available limits equal prudent borrowing. |
| failure modes | Revenue mistaken for receipts, annual prepayments fully spent on acquisition, omitted tax/refunds, a forecast shorter than the cash cycle and unverified borrowing availability. |
| repair actions | Correct receipt/payment timing first; renegotiate terms, slow commitments or address collection where justified. Preserve customer/value decisions when the problem is funding timing. |
| business-model dependencies | Stock-heavy ecommerce and receivable-heavy consulting differ from prepaid subscriptions; a negative cash cycle can help liquidity while still leaving future service and refund obligations. |

### P18: operations / capacity

**Related capabilities:** C11, C15, C20.  
**Source finding:** Constraint methods examine scarce resources and their shifting limits; professional fee management and multitask theory add workload and quality considerations beyond raw throughput. Sources: [S24](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s24), [S32](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s32), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42), [S43](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s43).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide what limits reliable fulfilment now and whether to change scheduling, scope, process, staffing or intake while protecting quality and existing obligations. |
| inputs | Actual demand, work content, rework, waiting, resource availability, skills, approved service promises, support and sales time, and current improvement hypotheses. |
| cheap evidence | Observe a bounded fulfilment cycle and compare required versus available resource time; test one reversible process change before adding capacity everywhere. |
| working artefacts | Flow/capacity account, bottleneck evidence, quality risks, intervention alternatives and a before/after review with competing explanations. |
| metrics | Completed acceptable work, lead time, queue/backlog, rework, utilisation with an explicit denominator and slack for variation; utilisation alone is not the objective. |
| commitment points | Approve increased intake, staffing or service levels. Stop promising additional work that cannot be fulfilled; investigate material safety/quality issues before commercial expansion. |
| failure modes | Sales exceed fulfilment, every resource kept busy while the bottleneck starves, founder labour treated as unlimited, omitted rework and local speed at the cost of quality. |
| repair actions | Correct the actual limiting resource or upstream cause, then measure whether the constraint moved. Permit a system-level change when a local fix cannot restore feasibility. |
| business-model dependencies | Solo consulting must reserve sales/admin/recovery time; physical delivery has inventory/logistics limits; software service capacity includes support, infrastructure and external providers. |

### P19: service design

**Related capabilities:** C02, C03, C10, C11.  
**Source finding:** The Double Diamond distinguishes problem discovery from solution development and delivery. Interview guidance and consulting standards support customer/context and operational responsibility. Sources: [S02](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s02), [S25](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s25), [S31](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s31).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose how a service journey delivers the promised outcome across customer actions, staff work and external dependencies rather than optimising one visible touchpoint. |
| inputs | Customer and operator observations, desired outcome, journey boundaries, handoffs, failure/recovery evidence, accessibility needs and approved service constraints. |
| cheap evidence | Observe a bounded journey and prototype the uncertain interaction or handoff; include the backstage work needed to make the apparent customer improvement real. |
| working artefacts | Journey and responsibility map, service hypothesis, problem/solution alternatives, prototype observations and a delivery-feasibility review. |
| metrics | Outcome/task completion, effort, waiting, handoff failures, rework and time-to-value; separate reported delight from the ability to repeatedly deliver. |
| commitment points | Approve tests with participants and new service promises. Specialist design/engineering teams own implementation; the business brief does not certify accessibility or technical feasibility. |
| failure modes | Polished frontstage with unworkable operations, designing from an imagined persona, ignoring non-users, and treating a prototype preference as evidence of paid, scalable delivery. |
| repair actions | Repair the failed journey segment or handoff; return to problem discovery if evidence rejects the underlying need. Preserve effective touchpoints and established obligations. |
| business-model dependencies | Human services involve co-production; digital products may depend on support or integrations; regulated or vulnerable-user journeys require stronger consent and specialist review. |

### P20: subscription businesses

**Related capabilities:** C05, C12, C15, C16.  
**Source finding:** Subscription evidence distinguishes ongoing collection from deliberate renewal; cohort heterogeneity and provider-specific billing definitions complicate lifetime and retention interpretation. Sources: [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S26](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s26), [S27](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s27), [S45](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s45).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose a recurring value and payment arrangement whose renewal economics and customer choice remain viable beyond initial acquisition. |
| inputs | Recurring customer need, billing/renewal terms, active use, cohorts, discounts, support/delivery costs, failed payments, cancellation behaviour and remaining service obligations. |
| cheap evidence | Examine an existing renewal cohort and cancellation/support cases; test a bounded onboarding, value or term-comprehension change before scaling acquisition. |
| working artefacts | Recurring-value hypothesis, renewal/exit journey, cohort revenue and activity reconciliation, cost/cash scenario and evidence-qualified retention assumptions. |
| metrics | Customer, revenue and activity retention, gross versus net revenue retention with fixed-cohort definitions, voluntary/involuntary churn and cash contribution; no universal healthy percentage. |
| commitment points | Approve recurring terms, reminders, guarantees and cancellation changes. Treat annual cash as funding paired with obligations, not immediate unconstrained profit. |
| failure modes | Inertia interpreted as product value, hidden renewal, expansion masking widespread losses, free-to-paid assumptions imported from another segment and annual cash masking future costs. |
| repair actions | Fix value delivery, term clarity or billing errors at their owner. Make cancellation honest rather than harder; reassess pricing only if its evidence or economics is responsible. |
| business-model dependencies | Consumer apps, enterprise SaaS and recurring services have different renewal units, stakeholders and costs. Usage or add-ons may coexist but do not erase the core recurring obligation. |

### P21: marketplaces

**Related capabilities:** C01, C06, C07, C14, C19.  
**Source finding:** Two-sided-market theory emphasises cross-side participation and price allocation; experiment models show that participant interference can alter measurement bias. Sources: [S28](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s28), [S29](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s29), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide how a bounded set of buyers and suppliers can transact usefully and sustainably, including which side is constrained and how charges affect both sides. |
| inputs | Buyer and supplier roles, matching geography/category, availability, transaction trust, prices/fees, participant alternatives, fulfilment ownership and cross-side effects. |
| cheap evidence | Test a narrow manually coordinated market with honest participant expectations; observe matching/fulfilment before building marketplace infrastructure or assuming network effects. |
| working artefacts | Two-sided value and incentive map, matching/availability evidence, fee and contribution model, trust/resolution responsibilities and an experiment-design rationale. |
| metrics | Match/fulfilment rate, time to match, repeat participation on each side, failed transactions, net take revenue and contribution; gross merchandise value is not platform revenue. |
| commitment points | Approve participant commitments, fees and data sharing. Platform experiments must consider spillovers; a customer-randomised test is not automatically an unbiased full-market estimate. |
| failure modes | One side grows without useful availability on the other, excessive fees reduce participation, trust/fulfilment failures, infrastructure before liquidity and naive treatment/control interference. |
| repair actions | Correct the constrained side, matching friction or fee/incentive conflict. Preserve functioning counterparties; change system-level design when the cross-side dependency requires it. |
| business-model dependencies | Local services, product resale and digital matching have different search, trust and fulfilment economics. A theoretical network effect is not proof that a new marketplace has one. |

### P22: SaaS

**Related capabilities:** C03, C05, C11, C12, C15.  
**Source finding:** Billing definitions, cohort models and experiment-interpretation research provide distinct views of SaaS performance; no one dashboard number establishes product-market-business-model fit. Sources: [S18](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s18), [S27](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s27), [S36](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s36), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose a software revenue/delivery arrangement and diagnose whether acquisition, activation, continued value, expansion or service cost is the binding commercial issue. |
| inputs | Account/user/payer roles, activation/outcome events, recurring or usage pricing, cohorts, infrastructure and support costs, billing definitions and enterprise dependencies. |
| cheap evidence | Inspect one account cohort’s path from acquisition to useful adoption and renewal; test the missing value step before expanding a sales funnel. |
| working artefacts | Account/value-path account, pricing-unit rationale, recurring/usage metric dictionary, cohort contribution scenario and commercial requirements for software/design teams. |
| metrics | Time to useful adoption, account and user retention, fixed-cohort expansion/contraction, service cost per meaningful unit and realised payback; MRR is neither cash nor profit. |

### P23: ecommerce

**Related capabilities:** C04, C05, C10, C11, C15, C16.  
**Source finding:** Return-policy evidence and inventory/working-capital guidance link conversion to fulfilment, returns and cash. Permission to accept an order is not evidence it can be delivered. Sources: [S11](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s11), [S22](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s22), [S30](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s30).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Choose an offer, stock commitment or conversion improvement that remains viable after fulfilment, returns, payment and inventory exposure. |
| inputs | Product/variant availability by location, supplier and shipping terms, demand uncertainty, landed costs, return/refund behaviour, advertised terms and usable cash. |
| cheap evidence | Reconcile a small order-to-delivery-to-return sample and physical stock where applicable; test demand within honest availability limits before buying a large assortment. |
| working artefacts | Order-economics and inventory/cash account, return-policy comparison, fulfilment promises, stockout/preorder assumptions and a bounded commitment recommendation. |
| metrics | Net retained orders, contribution after delivery/returns, stockout and fulfilment outcomes, inventory exposure and cash cycle; gross checkout sales alone are insufficient. |
| commitment points | Approve purchase orders, preorder promises, discounts and return terms. Actual fulfilment operators own inventory and shipment updates; do not automate stock commitments from a forecast. |
| failure modes | Overselling without supply confidence, artificial scarcity, uncosted free returns/shipping, discount-led demand that destroys margin and excess stock consuming working capital. |
| repair actions | Correct the stock, delivery promise or return-cost assumption first. Adjust purchasing or offer scope proportionately; do not rewrite brand/customer decisions for an inventory-record defect. |
| business-model dependencies | Owned inventory, dropshipped and made-to-order models distribute control and risk differently. Perishable or regulated products add constraints requiring appropriate specialist evidence. |

### P24: professional services

**Related capabilities:** C04, C05, C10, C11, C16.  
**Source finding:** Consulting standards emphasise client value and professional conduct; fee-management guidance connects scope and recovery. A high quoted rate does not prove profitable capacity use. Sources: [S31](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s31), [S32](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s32), [S20](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s20), [S42](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s42).

**Project application**

|---|---|
| decision it supports | Select a deliverable engagement scope, fee structure and client load consistent with competence, available time, cash timing and the client’s intended outcome. |

### P25: experimentation / A/B testing

**Related capabilities:** C17, C18, C19.  
**Source finding:** Statistical guidance makes design depend on the question and error budget; sequential research permits valid monitoring. Entrepreneurship trials test a method, not every small experiment tactic. Sources: [S33](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s33), [S34](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s34), [S35](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s35), [S36](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s36), [S37](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s37).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide which uncertain assumption to test and whether its results justify continuation, change or termination; choose the least costly adequate design, not simply the smallest sample. |
| inputs | Hypothesis and alternative explanations, target population, unit of assignment, outcome and guardrails, relevant effect size, precision/power needs, timing and ethical constraints. |
| cheap evidence | Check instrumentation and exposure first. Use interviews or prototypes for mechanisms and randomised comparisons for suitable causal questions; retain uncertainty when resources cannot identify the effect. |
| working artefacts | Pre-result decision rule, design/assignment rationale, measurement definitions, permitted stopping/analysis plan, observed results, deviations and a learning decision. |
| metrics | Effect size and interval, sample/exposure, validity checks, guardrail outcomes and decision consequences; a non-significant result is not automatic equivalence or proof of failure. |
| commitment points | Approve external exposure, data and spend. Fix the analysis/stopping approach before interpreting results; a decision under uncertainty is not a newly validated empirical claim. |
| failure modes | Peeking with fixed-horizon tests, outcome switching, mixed cohorts, multiple changes without identification, underpowered claims, selection bias and a test that cannot change the decision. |
| repair actions | Correct the design or instrument at its owner; rerun when needed rather than rewriting the hypothesis after observing results. Preserve genuine negative or inconclusive findings. |
| business-model dependencies | Long sales cycles need delayed outcomes; rare events limit evidence; marketplace interference needs different designs; safety-critical contexts can require much stronger safeguards and expertise. |

### P26: product-market fit concepts

**Related capabilities:** C01, C02, C12, C15, C21.  
**Source finding:** The Superhuman account offers a selected-user survey heuristic; broader business-model work requires coherence beyond customer enthusiasm. Neither defines a universal pass threshold. Sources: [S05](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s05), [S37](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s37), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39), [S40](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s40).

**Project application**

| Required field | Application and boundary |
|---|---|
| decision it supports | Decide whether observed demand, continued delivered value and business-model feasibility justify the next commitment, while stating the particular meaning of fit being assessed. |
| inputs | Target and observed customer populations, alternatives, usage and renewal evidence, willingness to pay, contribution/capacity/cash, non-user or lost-customer evidence and uncertainty. |
| cheap evidence | Review several aligned signals from a bounded real cohort; use a disappointment survey to explore perceived value, not to substitute for retention or economic evidence. |
| working artefacts | Fit assessment by separate dimensions, population/selection explanation, conflicting signals, decision-relevant unknowns and the next discriminating test. |
| metrics | Appropriate continued use, voluntary retention, purchase/renewal and contribution evidence; survey thresholds remain source-specific heuristics rather than universal success criteria. |
| commitment points | Owner approval for scale or strategic commitment. Do not declare product-market fit solely because a selected subgroup crosses a disappointment threshold. |

### P27: business-model innovation

**Related capabilities:** C01, C06, C17, C20, C21.  
**Source finding:** Theory-led critiques warn against searching only where feedback is easy; replication evidence supports disciplined testing. Business-model integration adds stakeholder and system-level coherence. Sources: [S06](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s06), [S28](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s28), [S37](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s37), [S38](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s38), [S39](2026-09-10-stage-03-evidence-and-terminology.md#s39).

## 3. What this research changes, and what it preserves

The map preserves Stage 1’s ownership, human approval, professional boundaries and multidimensional quality. It preserves Stage 2’s source attributions as historical extraction, rather than silently rewriting what the authors said. Its independent research supplies reasons to qualify or reject universal interpretations. The [claims and principles review](2026-09-10-stage-03-claims-failures-and-principles.md) records those dispositions and remaining uncertainty.

Additional emphases discovered outside the initial marketing-heavy frame include causal incrementality versus attribution, provider-specific denominator semantics, active choice versus passive subscription billing, treatment responsiveness versus risk prediction, two-sided experiment interference and the tension between easily tested feedback and theory-led innovation. These are useful analytical distinctions, not grounds for a new universal ontology, CRM, optimisation engine or multi-agent executive team.

The next stage consumes the customer/buyer/value distinctions, connected business responsibilities, evidence meanings and model dependencies from these cards. It does not inherit a claim that a particular market, customer, price, channel, growth threshold or tool has been validated for any real project.
