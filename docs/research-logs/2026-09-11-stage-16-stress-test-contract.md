## 1. Purpose and evidence boundary

Stage 16 tests whether one business-building architecture can reason across three deliberately different structures without becoming a consumer-subscription playbook, a consultancy playbook or an open-source monetisation playbook. The canonical cases reuse the accepted **synthetic** E13–E15 Level-5 fixtures from Stage 15 and add the exact adversarial proposals required by §23. They are not statements about live Kakeibo, a live consultancy, or actual Production Skills repositories.

The stress test is a design test. The current repository does not yet contain installed production skills, so this stage does not fabricate host-agent runs. Instead it executes deterministic arithmetic and contract checks against the fixed fixtures, and performs a complete semantic review of every adversarial case. Later Stage 17 owns the general benchmark architecture; Stages 22–24 own installed implementation and clean-install execution.

## 2. Common response contract

Every canonical stress response must:

1. identify the bounded decision and relevant accepted versions;
2. distinguish supplied observation, scenario, assumption, metric, interpretation and decision;
3. preserve customer rights, truthful claims and professional handoffs independently of commercial upside;
4. check delivery quality/capacity, economics, retention/downstream quality and dated usable cash where relevant;
5. keep external execution authority separate from a recommendation or tool capability;
6. preserve accepted decisions unless evidence implicates them;
7. identify the smallest responsible repair, including a justified coupled change only where dependencies require it;
8. retain unresolved facts as **BLOCKED**, contradicted proposals as **FAIL**, and bounded supported analysis as **PASS**;
9. reject a one-number business score or a favourable metric that averages away a blocking gate; and
10. keep legal, tax, investment and other professional conclusions with their qualified owners.

These invariants are identical across A, B and C. Business-model-specific facts change; the core evidence/decision discipline does not.

## 3. Canonical structure and execution

Each stress-test document contains the original §23 exercise list, a self-contained canonical prompt, a bounded positive control and the five exact adversarial cases. For each adversarial case the record states the proposed shortcut, expected disposition, accepted decisions to preserve and smallest sufficient repair. The fixture JSON is the calculation/control-flow source for the local verifier.

The verifier checks exact test names, exercise coverage, adversarial coverage, immutable baseline versions, numeric reconciliations and negative controls. It is not a legal classifier or an LLM evaluation. Semantic sufficiency is reviewed in the conformance record case-by-case.

## 4. Cross-structure survival test

The architecture survives Stage 16 only if all three conditions hold together:

- **Structure specificity:** each fixture exercises its own economics, actors, channels, delivery and constraints rather than being relabelled marketing examples.
- **Core invariance:** the same evidence, truthfulness, capacity, economics, cash, authority and preservation rules continue to apply without a special universal ontology or new executive-agent layer.
- **Bounded repair:** every adversarial proposal can be rejected or blocked while preserving unrelated accepted choices and naming the smallest evidence/repair path.

No canonical test is passed merely because its arithmetic is correct. Conversely, a failing commercial proposal can be a successful stress-test outcome when the architecture identifies the right failure and refuses unnecessary redesign.
