### R01 — CMA, Unfair commercial practices (CMA207)

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices

**Status:** UK regulatory guidance; publication page updated 18 November 2025.

**Material inspected:** HTML sections on consumer scope, vulnerability, misleading practices and banned practices, including limited-time claims. Not a review of every cited enactment.

**Finding retained:** The guidance distinguishes prohibited practices, misleading information and context-dependent consumer effects. Its limited-time discussion differentiates false limits from genuine ones.

**Limit and project use:** Use to identify questions and evidence, not pronounce on an unspecified trader or assume every B2B transaction is covered. Source examples and penalty figures are not reproduced.

<a id="r02"></a>

### R02 — CAP Code, Section 3: Misleading advertising

**Source:** https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html

**Status:** UK non-broadcast advertising code; live page accessed on the review date.

**Material inspected:** HTML rules on substantiation, qualifications, comparisons, prices, reviews and guarantees.

**Finding retained:** Objective claims need appropriate prior evidence; qualifications must not contradict the claim. Comparison and guarantee representations have additional conditions.

**Limit and project use:** A code rule is not a universal statute, independent commercial validation, or proof that a particular advertisement is acceptable. Broadcast and other jurisdictions require their own review.

<a id="r03"></a>

### R03 — CMA, Short guide: publishing consumer reviews

**Source:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews/short-guide-for-businesses-publishing-consumer-reviews-and-complying-with-consumer-protection-law

**Status:** UK guidance associated with CMA208, published 4 April 2025.

**Material inspected:** HTML short guide; not the linked 29-page PDF.

**Finding retained:** Review publishers need arrangements addressing fake and concealed incentivised reviews, including prevention and removal.

**Limit and project use:** A genuine customer account does not independently substantiate every objective claim inside it. Do not infer that every incentivised review is automatically prohibited or that disclosure cures fabrication.

### R04 — ICO, PECR electronic-mail marketing rules

**Source:** https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-direct-marketing-using-electronic-mail/how-do-we-comply-with-the-pecr-electronic-mail-marketing-rules/

**Status:** UK guidance, including 2026 updates.

**Material inspected:** HTML distinctions between solicited and unsolicited messages, subscriber types, consent, soft opt-in, identity and opt-out.

**Finding retained:** Individual and corporate subscriber rules differ. Exceptions have conditions; data-protection obligations remain a separate consideration.

**Limit and project use:** Neither a public business address nor a general legitimate-interest assertion establishes permission for every campaign. The charitable-purpose exception is not a commercial-sender default. No live list or consent record was inspected.

### R05 — ICO, Storage and access technologies: exceptions

**Source:** https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/

**Status:** Current UK detailed guidance, accessed 10 September 2026.

**Material inspected:** HTML five-exception overview and purpose, information and objection conditions, especially statistical purposes and strictly necessary processing.

**Finding retained:** The page describes five exceptions. Statistical-purpose and appearance exceptions include information and easy-objection requirements; exceptions are narrow and purpose-dependent.

**Limit and project use:** Do not impose an obsolete two-exception checklist or exempt every analytics product. Advertising use, third-party purposes and personal-data processing require separate examination.
