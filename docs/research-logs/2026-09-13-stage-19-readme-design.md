### 4.2 Bounded test and decision note

The fixed fee loses contribution on the five-hour record. The middle case alone cannot decide the price structure. Retain both as draft options while learning about scope/price comprehension and real buyer response; hourly pricing is a defensible test candidate for workload protection, not a demonstrated buyer preference or universally superior price.

Compare three methods. Existing-job calculation is adequate for the supplied cost exposure and was executed, but cannot reveal buyer comprehension or demand. A large landing-page or paid price experiment adds acquisition cost and confounding before the quote itself is understood. Select a small moderated quote-comprehension test as the cheapest adequate next design for the specific misunderstanding risk. It is formative, not a population preference estimate or paid-demand test.

Proposed test: after permission and reviewed truthful scope/terms, recruit five eligible, consenting potential buyers within a seven-day window. Show both clearly hypothetical quote forms with the same report, scope, proof and proposed payment terms; alternate presentation order and record it. Ask each person to explain what work is included, what they would owe and what happens if scope changes. Freeze the materials and rule before any session. No selling, charging or price change is authorised by this design.

Supportive outcome: all five completed participants correctly explain both price structures and the scope/variation obligations, with no material misunderstanding; the next decision is whether to authorise a small real-quote test, not to claim willingness to pay. Adverse outcome: any material misunderstanding triggers repair of the implicated disclosure and retest before use. Inconclusive outcome: incomplete recruitment, missing responses, inconsistent materials or unresolved comprehension leaves the claim unresolved; do not record missing observations as failures or successes. Stop immediately for misleading terms, permission withdrawal or a data-use issue. Sampling, order effects, hypothetical answers and a five-person formative group limit inference. This proposed decision rule is local to the rehearsal, not a universal business threshold. No test results were collected.
